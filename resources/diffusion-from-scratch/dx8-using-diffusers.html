Using pretrained diffusion models in practice: pipelines, schedulers, memory, parameters and workflows

Training your own model teaches the theory; most real work uses pretrained ones. This part shows the moving parts of a modern text-to-image pipeline, the settings that matter and why, how to fit big models on small GPUs, and how to run controlled, repeatable experiments. The components of a text-to-image pipeline

A modern latent diffusion pipeline (Stable Diffusion family) is five replaceable parts:

ComponentJobTypical sizeSwap for
Tokenizer and text encoderTurn the prompt into embeddings (CLIP ViT-L, OpenCLIP, T5-XXL in newer models)0.1 to 5 GBLarger encoders improve prompt following
DenoiserPredict noise (or velocity) given the noisy latent, the timestep and the text embeddings1 to 12 GBU-Net (SD 1.x, SDXL) or Diffusion Transformer (SD3, Flux)
Scheduler (sampler)Update rule from one latent to the next (DX2)noneDDIM, Euler, DPM++ 2M, UniPC, LCM
VAEEncode images to latents and decode latents to images (DX3)0.3 GBFine-tuned VAEs fix colour and fine detail
Safety checker / filtersOptional output screeningsmallPlatform policy

The latent has 1/8 the spatial resolution of the image and 4 or 16 channels, so a 1024 x 1024 image is denoised as a 128 x 128 latent.

def latent_shape(width, height, channels=4, factor=8): return (channels, height // factor, width // factor)
def latent_elements(width, height, channels=4): c, h, w = latent_shape(width, height, channels); return c * h * w
for w, h in [(512, 512), (768, 768), (1024, 1024), (1536, 1024)]:
    c, lh, lw = latent_shape(w, h)
    print(f"{w}x{h} image -> latent {c}x{lh}x{lw} = {latent_elements(w, h):,} numbers ({w * h * 3 / latent_elements(w, h):.0f}x fewer than the pixels)")

Output

768x768 image -> latent 4x96x96 = 36,864 numbers (48x fewer than the pixels)
1024x1024 image -> latent 4x128x128 = 65,536 numbers (48x fewer than the pixels)
1536x1024 image -> latent 4x128x192 = 98,304 numbers (48x fewer than the pixels)

How this gets asked

What does the scheduler do in a diffusion pipeline?

  • Encodes the prompt That is the text encoder.
  • Defines how the denoiser's prediction is turned into the next latent (the sampling update rule) Correct.
  • Decodes the image That is the VAE.
  • Filters unsafe images No.

You can swap schedulers without retraining.

A 1024 x 1024 image is denoised as a latent with spatial size 128 x 128. What is the downsampling factor of the VAE?

1024 / 128 = 8.

Self-check

List the parts of a latent diffusion pipeline.

Tokenizer and text encoder, denoiser (U-Net or DiT), scheduler, VAE (and optional safety filter).

The diffusers library by example

These snippets are shown, not run. They are the standard shape of Hugging Face diffusers code.

import torch
from diffusers import StableDiffusionXLPipeline, DPMSolverMultistepScheduler

pipe = StableDiffusionXLPipeline.from_pretrained(
    "stabilityai/stable-diffusion-xl-base-1.0",
    torch_dtype=torch.float16,          # half precision halves memory and speeds up inference
    use_safetensors=True,
)
pipe.scheduler = DPMSolverMultistepScheduler.from_config(pipe.scheduler.config)   # swap the sampler
pipe.to("cuda")

generator = torch.Generator("cuda").manual_seed(1234)                              # reproducibility
image = pipe(
    prompt="a lighthouse on a cliff at sunset, oil painting, detailed",
    negative_prompt="blurry, low quality, watermark",
    num_inference_steps=25,
    guidance_scale=6.5,
    width=1024, height=1024,
    generator=generator,
).images[0]
image.save("lighthouse.png")

Guidance scale: the dial that matters most

Classifier-free guidance runs the denoiser twice per step, once with the prompt and once without, and extrapolates: eps = eps_uncond + w x (eps_cond - eps_uncond). The weight w is the guidance scale (DX2).

def cfg(eps_uncond, eps_cond, w): return [u + w * (c - u) for u, c in zip(eps_uncond, eps_cond)]
u, c = [0.10, -0.20, 0.05], [0.30, -0.10, 0.25]
for w in (0, 1, 3, 7.5, 15):
    print(f"guidance {w:>4}: {[round(v, 2) for v in cfg(u, c, w)]}  (0 ignores the prompt, 1 is the plain conditional prediction, larger exaggerates the prompt direction)")
steps, cost_per_call = 25, 1.0
print(f"cost: with guidance every step needs 2 denoiser calls -> {2 * steps} calls instead of {steps}; distilled or guidance-free models skip the second call")

Effects of high w: stronger prompt adherence, less diversity, saturated colours, burnt-looking contrast and artefacts. Newer models are trained with guidance distillation and use scales near 1 to 4. Some pipelines use dynamic thresholding or rescaling to reduce oversaturation.

How this gets asked

What does a very high guidance scale usually cause?

  • Perfect realism No.
  • Strong prompt adherence but oversaturation, less diversity and artefacts Correct.
  • Faster sampling No.
  • Better text rendering always No.

Typical values are 5 to 8 for non-distilled models.

Why does classifier-free guidance double the compute per step?

  • It uses two GPUs No.
  • It evaluates the denoiser with and without the prompt and combines the predictions Correct.
  • It runs the VAE twice No.
  • It repeats the text encoder No.

Guidance-distilled models avoid the second pass.

Resolution and aspect ratio

Models are trained at specific sizes (SD 1.5 at 512, SDXL around 1024). Generating far from the native size produces duplicated subjects and odd compositions. Options: generate at native size and upscale with a dedicated model (ESRGAN, latent upscaler, tile-based diffusion upscaling), use hires fix (generate small, upsample the latent and refine with img2img at low strength), or use models trained with multiple aspect ratios.

Compute grows quickly with resolution: the number of latent tokens is proportional to the area, and self-attention cost grows with the square of the tokens, so doubling both sides multiplies attention cost by about 16.

def tokens(width, height, patch=2, factor=8): return (width // factor // patch) * (height // factor // patch)      # DiT-style latent patches
base = tokens(512, 512)
for w, h in [(512, 512), (768, 768), (1024, 1024), (2048, 2048)]:
    t = tokens(w, h)
    print(f"{w}x{h}: {t:>6,} tokens | attention cost relative to 512x512: {(t / base) ** 2:>7.0f}x | linear parts: {t / base:>4.0f}x")

Output

768x768:  2,304 tokens | attention cost relative to 512x512:       5x | linear parts:    2x
1024x1024:  4,096 tokens | attention cost relative to 512x512:      16x | linear parts:    4x

How this gets asked

Why does generating far above a model's native resolution often duplicate subjects?

  • The GPU runs out of memory That causes errors, not duplicates.
  • The model never saw such layouts in training, so it tiles content to fill the canvas Correct.
  • The scheduler fails No.
  • The VAE breaks No.

Use hires fix or upscalers instead.

Latency estimate

Total time is about steps x passes_per_step x time_per_denoiser_call + VAE decode + text encode.

def seconds(steps, passes, ms_per_call, vae_ms=150, encode_ms=40): return (steps * passes * ms_per_call + vae_ms + encode_ms) / 1000
configs = [("SDXL, 30 steps, CFG", 30, 2, 90), ("SDXL, 20 steps, DPM++, CFG", 20, 2, 90), ("distilled Turbo, 4 steps, no CFG", 4, 1, 90), ("single-step distilled", 1, 1, 90)]
for name, steps, passes, ms in configs:
    print(f"{name:>34}: {seconds(steps, passes, ms):5.2f} s per image on a GPU taking {ms} ms per denoiser call")

Schedulers and step counts in practice

SchedulerCharacterTypical steps
DDPM (ancestral, stochastic)Faithful but slow250 to 1000
DDIMDeterministic, supports editing and inversion20 to 50
Euler / Euler aSimple ODE or SDE steps; "a" adds noise each step (varied results, does not converge to one image as steps grow)20 to 40
DPM++ 2M (Karras)Fast, high-order, strong default20 to 30
DPM++ SDEStochastic, detailed, slower20 to 30
UniPCHigh quality at very low step counts10 to 20
LCM / Turbo / Lightning schedulersMatch distilled models1 to 8
Flow-matching Euler (SD3, Flux)Straight-path ODE20 to 50, or about 4 for distilled variants

A good method: fix the seed and prompt, vary one setting at a time, and compare grids. More steps improve quality up to a plateau, after which they only waste time (and ancestral samplers keep changing the image).

How this gets asked

What distinguishes 'Euler a' (ancestral) from plain Euler?

  • It is deterministic The opposite.
  • It injects fresh noise each step, so results keep changing as steps increase and do not converge to one image Correct.
  • It is slower to import No.
  • It needs no seed No.

Use non-ancestral solvers for reproducible convergence.

Self-check

How many steps does a good solver need?

About 20 to 30 for DPM++ or UniPC, versus 1 to 8 for distilled models and hundreds for plain DDPM.

Prompting that works

  • Describe subject, setting, style, lighting, composition and medium concisely: "a ginger cat asleep on a windowsill, soft morning light, shallow depth of field, 35 mm photo".
  • Modern models with large text encoders understand natural sentences; older CLIP-based models respond to keyword lists and weighting syntax like (word:1.3).
  • Negative prompts help older models (remove "blurry, extra fingers"), and matter less for newer ones.
  • Seeds: explore with random seeds, then fix a good seed and refine the prompt. Keep a record of prompt, negative prompt, seed, scheduler, steps, scale, size and model version: that is a reproducible experiment.
  • Text in images is hard for small encoders; larger text encoders and newer models do better.
  • Compositional prompts (counts, spatial relations, attribute binding) remain error-prone; use regional prompting, layout conditioning (ControlNet, GLIGEN) or iterative editing.

A workflow for repeatable experiments

import json, hashlib
def run_card(model, prompt, negative, seed, scheduler, steps, cfg, width, height, lora=None):
    card = dict(model=model, prompt=prompt, negative=negative, seed=seed, scheduler=scheduler, steps=steps, cfg=cfg, size=[width, height], lora=lora)
    card["id"] = hashlib.sha1(json.dumps(card, sort_keys=True).encode()).hexdigest()[:10]       # identical settings give an identical id
    return card
a = run_card("sdxl-base-1.0", "a lighthouse at sunset", "blurry", 1234, "dpmpp_2m_karras", 25, 6.5, 1024, 1024)
b = run_card("sdxl-base-1.0", "a lighthouse at sunset", "blurry", 1234, "dpmpp_2m_karras", 25, 6.5, 1024, 1024)
c = run_card("sdxl-base-1.0", "a lighthouse at sunset", "blurry", 1235, "dpmpp_2m_karras", 25, 6.5, 1024, 1024)
print("same settings -> same id:", a["id"] == b["id"], "| a different seed -> different id:", a["id"] != c["id"])

Output

same settings -> same id: True | a different seed -> different id: True

Practical advice:

  1. Fix everything except one variable per comparison; generate several seeds per setting, because a single image can mislead.
  2. Judge with blind side-by-side comparisons, ideally by several people; add automatic metrics (CLIP score for prompt alignment, aesthetic predictors, FID for distributions, DX5) but trust them cautiously.
  3. Version everything: model hash, LoRA hashes, library versions (results can change between releases), and hardware (GPU kernels differ slightly).
  4. Be careful with licences and content: check each model's licence for commercial use, honour opt-outs, label AI-generated output where required, and keep safety filters on for user-facing products (DX5).
  5. Serving: batch requests, keep models warm in GPU memory, queue with priorities, cache text embeddings for repeated prompts, set time and resolution limits, and monitor GPU utilisation and queue latency (SD23).
Key idea

A pipeline is text encoder, denoiser, scheduler and VAE; you can swap each. Quality comes from the right native resolution, a good solver with 20 to 30 steps, a moderate guidance scale and a fixed, recorded seed. Fit large models on small GPUs with half precision, efficient attention, tiling, offload and quantisation, or use distilled few-step models, and treat every run as a reproducible, versioned experiment.

Interview questions: Diffusion in practice

14 questions. Try to answer aloud in under a minute, then reveal. Tags show the level.

Basic Name the components of a Stable Diffusion pipeline.

Tokenizer and text encoder, the denoiser (U-Net or transformer), the scheduler, and the VAE decoder (plus an optional safety checker).

Basic What does the guidance scale control?

How strongly the conditional prediction is exaggerated relative to the unconditional one: higher values follow the prompt more but reduce diversity and can oversaturate.

Basic Why does the same seed reproduce an image?

The seed fixes the initial noise; with the same prompt, model, scheduler, steps and settings the deterministic pipeline gives the same output.

Medium How do you run a large model on an 8 GB GPU?

Use fp16 or bf16, efficient attention, VAE tiling, CPU offload of idle components, quantised weights and distilled few-step models.

Medium What is a negative prompt and how does it work?

It replaces the empty prompt in the unconditional branch of classifier-free guidance, so the sample is pushed away from what it describes.

Medium How many sampling steps are typical?

20 to 30 with a good solver such as DPM++ or UniPC; 1 to 8 for distilled models; hundreds for plain DDPM.

Medium What is the difference between Euler and Euler ancestral samplers?

Ancestral samplers inject fresh noise each step, so results keep changing as steps increase and do not converge to a single image.

Medium Why do high resolutions beyond the training size produce duplicated subjects?

The model has not seen such compositions and tiles content; generate at native size and upscale or use hires fix.

Medium How do img2img strength and inpainting masks work?

Img2img adds noise up to a level set by strength then denoises; low strength keeps the layout. Inpainting regenerates masked regions and preserves the rest.

Advanced How does latency scale with settings?

Roughly steps x passes per step x time per denoiser call, plus VAE decode; guidance doubles passes, and attention cost grows with the square of the token count.

Advanced How would you serve a text-to-image model at scale?

Keep models warm in GPU memory, batch requests, queue with priorities, cache embeddings, enforce resolution and time limits, filter outputs and monitor utilisation and latency.

Advanced How do you make experiments reproducible?

Record model and LoRA versions, prompts, seed, scheduler, steps, guidance, size and library versions, and compare several seeds per setting.

Advanced What is hires fix?

Generate at a native low resolution, upsample the latent or image, and refine with img2img at moderate strength to add detail without composition errors.

Advanced Why combine several LoRAs carefully?

Adapters can conflict or amplify each other; weights must be tuned and combinations tested, and strong LoRAs degrade base model quality.
