Beyond images: video, audio, 3D, science, robotics and discrete diffusion

Diffusion is a recipe, not an image trick: corrupt data gradually, learn to reverse it. Wherever you can define noise and a network that handles the data's structure, the recipe applies. This part tours the major uses and the extra problems each one adds.

Advanced~3.5 hours

The recipe, restated

Any diffusion model needs four decisions:

  1. Data representation: pixels, latents, spectrograms, point clouds, atom coordinates, action sequences, tokens.
  2. Corruption process: Gaussian noise for continuous data; masking or token replacement for discrete data.
  3. Denoiser architecture: a network that respects the data's structure (convolution, transformer, graph network, equivariant network).
  4. Conditioning: text, class, another modality, a partial observation.

What four things must you decide to apply diffusion to a new modality?

  • Colour, size, speed, price No.

Why does diffusion generalise across modalities?

It only needs a corruption process and a denoiser matched to the data structure.

Video

The extra dimension

A video is a stack of frames, so a clip of 16 frames at 512 by 512 contains 16 times the data of an image, and frames must be temporally consistent: objects keep identity, motion is smooth, lighting does not flicker.

frames, seconds, fps = 16, 5, 24
latent_per_frame = 64 * 64 * 4
print("latent values for a 5 s clip at 24 fps:", seconds * fps * latent_per_frame)
print("a 16-frame clip:", frames * latent_per_frame)
print("naive full attention between all positions in 16 frames:", (frames * 64 * 64) ** 2)

Architectures

  • Inflated image models (Video LDM, AnimateDiff): take a pretrained image U-Net and insert temporal layers (temporal attention or 1D convolution along the time axis) that mix information across frames. Cheap, reuses image knowledge.
  • Space-time transformers (Sora-style): compress video with a 3D VAE into a latent; cut it into spacetime patches treated as tokens; a diffusion transformer attends across space and time. Handles variable duration, resolution and aspect ratio.
  • Cascades: generate low resolution, low frame-rate video, then upsample in space and time with separate models.
  • Conditioning: text, a first frame (image to video), a last frame, camera motion, depth, or a reference video.
ProblemTypical remedy
FlickerTemporal attention, noise shared across frames, consistency losses
Cost grows with durationFactorised space and time attention, latent compression, autoregressive extension of long videos
Physics and object permanenceScale, data quality; still a research frontier
EvaluationFVD (video Fréchet distance), human study, benchmark suites

What extra problem does video add?

Audio and speech

  • Spectrogram diffusion: represent audio as a mel spectrogram (an image of frequency versus time), diffuse it with a U-Net, then convert to waveform with a vocoder (HiFi-GAN or a diffusion vocoder such as WaveGrad and DiffWave).
  • Latent audio diffusion (Stable Audio, AudioLDM): compress with an audio autoencoder or neural codec and diffuse latents conditioned on text, duration and timing.
  • Text to speech: diffusion for acoustic features (Grad-TTS), or hybrid systems that predict codec tokens; voice cloning raises consent and fraud concerns.
  • Music: long-range structure is hard, so models combine local diffusion with global conditioning or autoregressive structure.
  • Speech enhancement and separation: condition on a noisy signal and denoise.

3D

3D training data is scarce compared with images, so a common approach borrows a 2D image prior.

Score distillation sampling (DreamFusion)

Optimise a 3D representation (a NeRF or 3D Gaussians) so that renderings from any camera angle look like samples of the text-conditioned image model:

  1. Render the current 3D model from a random camera.
  2. Add noise to the rendering, ask the diffusion model to predict the noise given the prompt.
  3. The difference between predicted and actual noise gives a gradient that is pushed back through the renderer into the 3D parameters (the diffusion model's Jacobian is skipped).
  4. Repeat from many views.

Pros: no 3D data. Cons: slow (tens of minutes), over-saturated colours, the "Janus problem" (multiple faces from multiple views).

Faster routes

  • Multi-view diffusion (Zero-1-to-3, MVDream, SV3D): fine-tune an image model to produce consistent views from a camera pose, then reconstruct 3D from the views.
  • Feed-forward 3D generators (LRM and successors): predict a 3D representation from one or a few images in seconds.
  • Direct 3D diffusion on point clouds, voxels, triplanes or meshes, as data grows.

How this gets asked

What does score distillation sampling optimise?

  • The text encoder No.
  • A 3D representation, so that renderings from any angle look like samples from the image diffusion model Correct.
  • The VAE No.
  • The learning rate No.

Gradients from the 2D diffusion prior flow through the renderer into 3D parameters.

Self-check

How can you get 3D from a 2D model?

Score distillation sampling, or multi-view diffusion followed by reconstruction, or feed-forward 3D models.

Science and engineering

Proteins and molecules

Atoms live in 3D, and a rotated or translated molecule is the same molecule, so the denoiser must be equivariant to rotations and translations (SE(3)). Examples: RFdiffusion designs protein backbones; AlphaFold 3 uses a diffusion module for structure prediction; DiffDock docks ligands; GeoDiff and others generate molecular conformations. Conditioning on binding targets or desired function guides design toward useful molecules. Experimental validation in the lab remains essential.

Weather, materials, medical imaging

Diffusion models produce ensembles of weather forecasts (GenCast), generate crystal structures, super-resolve and denoise MRI and CT, and synthesise training data. The probabilistic output is valuable when the truth is uncertain.

How this gets asked

Why must molecular diffusion denoisers be equivariant?

  • To run faster Not the reason.
  • Rotating or translating a molecule should not change what it is, so the model should respect those symmetries Correct.
  • To avoid noise No.
  • Because atoms are discrete No.

SE(3)-equivariant networks build the symmetry in.

Robotics and decision making

Diffusion policy

Treat a sequence of future actions as the data to generate, conditioned on the current observation (camera images, robot state). The denoiser (a temporal 1D U-Net or a transformer) outputs a multimodal distribution of action trajectories, which handles tasks with several valid ways to act (go left or right around an obstacle) where simple regression would average them into a bad action. Actions are predicted in chunks and executed with receding horizon replanning. Used for manipulation tasks learned from demonstrations.

Planning

Diffuser-style models generate whole trajectories with guidance toward high reward, treating planning as sampling.

How this gets asked

Why is diffusion good for robot policies?

  • It needs no data No.
  • It represents multimodal action distributions instead of averaging different valid choices Correct.
  • It removes cameras No.
  • It makes robots stronger No.

Regression to the mean could pick an action between two valid options and collide.

Discrete diffusion and text

Gaussian noise does not apply to tokens. Alternatives:

  • Masked (absorbing) diffusion: the forward process randomly masks tokens until all are masked; the model learns to unmask, trained with a cross-entropy-type loss. Sampling unmasks many tokens in parallel over several steps (MaskGIT, D3PM, MDLM, recent diffusion language models).
  • Multinomial diffusion: randomly replace tokens with uniform random ones.
  • Continuous embedding diffusion: add Gaussian noise to token embeddings, round at the end (Diffusion-LM).
import random
random.seed(5)
tokens = "diffusion models can also generate text".split()
def mask(tokens, p):
    return [t if random.random() > p else "[M]" for t in tokens]
for p in (0.0, 0.3, 0.7, 1.0):
    print("mask prob %.1f ->" % p, " ".join(mask(tokens, p)))

Output

mask prob 0.0 -> diffusion models can also generate text
mask prob 0.3 -> [M] models can also generate [M]
mask prob 0.7 -> [M] [M] [M] [M] [M] [M]
mask prob 1.0 -> [M] [M] [M] [M] [M] [M]

Compared with autoregressive models, discrete diffusion can generate in any order, fill in the middle (infilling) and decode many tokens per step, but autoregressive models still lead on quality at scale and exploit KV caching (AI/ML guide).

How this gets asked

  • Adding Gaussian noise to tokens That is continuous.
  • Randomly replacing tokens with a mask symbol until all are masked Correct.
  • Sorting tokens No.
  • Deleting the vocabulary No.

The model learns to unmask them.

Interview questions: Beyond images

10 questions. Try to answer aloud in under a minute, then reveal. Tags show the level.

Basic How is video generation different from image generation?

It adds a time dimension: much more data and the need for temporal consistency. Models add temporal attention or use space-time transformers over compressed video latents.

Basic How does diffusion generate audio?

Represent audio as a spectrogram or codec latent, diffuse it with a U-Net or transformer, and convert back to a waveform with a vocoder or decoder.

Medium What is score distillation sampling?

Optimise a 3D representation so renderings from random views are denoised well by a text-conditioned image diffusion model; the diffusion gradient flows through the renderer into 3D parameters.

Medium What is the Janus problem in text-to-3D?

Multiple faces appear on one object because the 2D prior does not know which view it is seeing; view-dependent prompts and multi-view models reduce it.

Medium Why use diffusion for robot policies?

It represents multimodal action distributions and generates action chunks conditioned on observations, instead of averaging different valid behaviours.

Medium How does diffusion handle discrete data like text?

Use a discrete corruption such as masking tokens (absorbing diffusion) or token replacement, or add Gaussian noise to embeddings and round at the end.

Advanced Why must protein diffusion models be equivariant?

A molecule's identity does not change when rotated or translated, so SE(3)-equivariant denoisers build in the symmetry and learn more efficiently.

Advanced Compare diffusion with GANs, VAEs and autoregressive models.

GANs sample in one step but are unstable and cover modes poorly; VAEs are fast but blurry alone; autoregressive models give exact likelihood and scale well but sample sequentially; diffusion is stable and flexible at the cost of multiple steps.

Advanced What is a spacetime patch?

A token covering a small region over a few frames of the compressed video latent; transformers over such tokens handle variable durations, sizes and aspect ratios.

Advanced Why is long video generation hard?

Compute grows with frames, errors accumulate over time, and the model must keep identity, physics and camera motion consistent; solutions include factorised attention, hierarchical generation and autoregressive extension.
