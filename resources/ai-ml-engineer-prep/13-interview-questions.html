Interview question bank

Questions you are likely to hear in AI/ML engineer interviews, from basics to advanced, each with a model answer you can say in under a minute. Filter by level and topic, search, or pull a random question to practice out loud.

BasicMediumAdvanced13 topics

How to practice: read the question, answer aloud (or write 3 bullet points), then reveal. Aim for structure: the definition, why it matters, one number or example, and one tradeoff. Each topic page links back here, and the deep explanations live on those pages.

ML foundations (20)

Basic What is the difference between supervised, unsupervised and self-supervised learning?

Supervised learns from labeled pairs (x, y). Unsupervised finds structure without labels (clustering, PCA). Self-supervised creates labels from the data itself, like predicting the next token, which is how LLMs are pretrained.

Basic What is overfitting and how do you detect it?

The model fits noise in the training data and generalizes poorly. Detect it by a large gap between training and validation performance, or validation loss rising while training loss keeps falling.

Basic Why do we need separate train, validation and test sets?

Train fits parameters, validation picks hyperparameters and models, and test gives one unbiased final estimate. Tuning on the test set makes its score optimistic.

Basic Explain precision and recall with an example.

In spam filtering, precision is the share of emails flagged as spam that really are spam, and recall is the share of all spam that got flagged. Raising the threshold usually raises precision and lowers recall.

Basic Why is accuracy misleading on imbalanced data?

With 1% positives, predicting all negative gives 99% accuracy while catching nothing. Use precision, recall, F1, PR-AUC or cost-weighted metrics.

Basic What does a learning rate do, and what happens if it is too high or too low?

It scales each gradient step. Too high overshoots and can diverge (loss spikes or NaN). Too low converges slowly or gets stuck.

Basic What is the difference between a parameter and a hyperparameter?

Parameters (weights) are learned during training. Hyperparameters (learning rate, depth, batch size, k) are set before or around training and tuned on validation data.

Basic What is cross-entropy loss and why is it used for classification?

The negative log probability assigned to the correct class. Minimizing it is maximum likelihood, it punishes confident mistakes heavily, and with softmax its gradient is simply p - y.

Medium Explain the bias-variance tradeoff.

Expected error splits into bias squared (wrong assumptions, underfitting), variance (sensitivity to the training sample, overfitting) and irreducible noise. More capacity lowers bias but raises variance. Regularization and more data manage the balance.

Medium L1 vs L2 regularization: what is the difference in effect?

L1 adds lambda times the sum of |w| and drives some weights to exactly zero (sparse, feature selection). L2 adds lambda times the sum of w squared and shrinks all weights smoothly. AdamW applies decoupled L2-style weight decay.

Medium What is data leakage? Give two examples.

Information at training time that would not be available at prediction time, or test data influencing training. Examples: fitting a scaler on the full dataset before splitting, and duplicate users or documents across train and test.

Medium How does k-fold cross-validation work and when would you use it?

Split the data into k folds, train k times holding out a different fold each time, and average the scores. Use it when data is small, to get a more reliable estimate. Use group or time-based folds when rows are related.

Medium What does ROC-AUC measure? When would you prefer PR-AUC?

AUC is the probability a random positive scores higher than a random negative, so it is threshold-free. With rare positives, ROC can look optimistic because the FPR denominator is huge. PR-AUC focuses on positive-class performance.

Medium Explain gradient descent vs SGD vs Adam.

GD uses the full-data gradient. SGD or mini-batch uses noisy batch gradients (cheaper, can escape sharp minima). Adam keeps moving averages of the gradient and squared gradient, giving per-parameter adaptive step sizes with bias correction.

Medium What is the vanishing gradient problem and how is it addressed?

Gradients shrink exponentially through many layers with saturating activations, so early layers stop learning. Fixes: ReLU-family activations, residual connections, normalization layers, careful initialization.

Medium Bagging vs boosting?

Bagging trains models independently on bootstrap samples and averages them, which reduces variance (random forest). Boosting trains models sequentially, each fixing the previous errors, which reduces bias (XGBoost, LightGBM).

Medium How would you handle a heavily imbalanced classification dataset?

Use the right metric (PR-AUC, recall at fixed precision), class weights or resampling, threshold tuning for the business cost, stratified splits, and possibly anomaly-detection framing. Collect more positives if possible.

Advanced What is calibration and how do you fix a poorly calibrated classifier?

A model is calibrated if events predicted at 0.8 happen about 80% of the time. Check with reliability diagrams or ECE. Fix with Platt scaling (logistic on scores), isotonic regression or temperature scaling, fitted on held-out data.

Advanced Explain double descent.

As model capacity grows past the point where it exactly fits the training data, test error can fall again after the classic U-shaped rise. Large overparameterized networks generalize well despite interpolating, so the classic tradeoff curve is incomplete.

Advanced Covariate shift vs concept drift: how would you detect and handle each?

Covariate shift: P(x) changes. Detect it with input distribution tests or embedding drift, and reweight or retrain. Concept drift: P(y|x) changes. Detect it via degrading labeled metrics or delayed feedback, and retrain on recent labels or use online updates.

Transformers (17)

Basic What problem does self-attention solve compared with RNNs?

Every token can directly attend to every other token in one step (no long sequential chain), which captures long-range dependencies better and parallelizes across the sequence during training.

Basic What are Q, K and V in attention?

Learned projections of each token: the query says what a token is looking for, the key what it offers, the value what it passes along. Weights come from softmax(QK^T / sqrt(d_k)), and the output is the weighted sum of V.

Basic Why do transformers need positional encodings?

Self-attention is permutation-invariant, so without position information word order is lost. Positions are added (sinusoidal or learned) or injected into attention (RoPE, ALiBi).

Basic What is an embedding?

A learned dense vector representing a token (or item) such that similar meanings are close in vector space. Token embeddings are rows of a vocab x d table.

Basic Encoder-only vs decoder-only vs encoder-decoder: when do you use each?

Encoder-only (BERT) for understanding tasks, embeddings and rerankers. Decoder-only (GPT, Llama) for generation and chat. Encoder-decoder (T5) for sequence-to-sequence tasks like translation.

Medium Why divide by sqrt(d_k) in attention?

Dot products of random unit-variance vectors have variance d_k. Large scores saturate the softmax and kill gradients. Scaling restores unit variance.

Medium What is multi-head attention and why use it?

Several attention operations in parallel on lower-dimensional projections (d split across heads), concatenated and projected by W_O. Different heads can capture different relations (syntax, coreference, position).

Medium What is the role of residual connections and layer normalization?

Residuals (x + f(x)) give gradients a direct path and let layers learn refinements. Normalization keeps activations well scaled. Pre-norm placement stabilizes deep training.

Medium What is the causal mask?

A mask adding -inf to attention scores for future positions before the softmax, so token i only attends to tokens up to i. It makes next-token training valid and enables parallel teacher forcing.

Medium What is the computational complexity of self-attention?

O(n^2 d) compute and O(n^2) memory for the score matrix per head, for sequence length n. FlashAttention keeps compute exact but avoids materializing the n x n matrix in HBM.

Medium Explain RoPE.

Rotary position embedding rotates each 2-D pair of query and key dimensions by an angle proportional to position, so q_m . k_n depends on m - n (relative position). Context can be extended by rescaling frequencies (position interpolation, NTK, YaRN).

Medium What is the difference between LayerNorm and RMSNorm?

LayerNorm subtracts the mean and divides by the standard deviation, with a learned scale and bias. RMSNorm divides by the root mean square only (no centering), which is cheaper and works as well. Llama uses RMSNorm.

Medium How many parameters are in a transformer layer, roughly?

For MHA with a 4d MLP: about 4d^2 attention + 8d^2 MLP = 12d^2. For Llama 2 7B (d = 4096, SwiGLU 11,008) it is 202M per layer, and 32 layers plus embeddings gives 6.74B.

Advanced What is mixture of experts and what are its tradeoffs?

MLP layers are replaced by many experts, with a router sending each token to the top-k. Compute scales with active parameters, memory with total parameters. Tradeoffs: load balancing, communication (expert parallelism), and higher memory per unit of compute.

Advanced MQA vs GQA vs MHA: what changes and why does it matter?

They differ in the number of K/V heads: MHA has one per query head, GQA groups query heads to share K/V heads, MQA has a single K/V head. Fewer KV heads shrink the KV cache and memory bandwidth at decode, with usually small quality loss for GQA.

Advanced How does FlashAttention speed up attention without approximation?

It tiles Q, K, V into on-chip SRAM blocks and computes softmax incrementally (online softmax with running max and sum), never writing the n x n matrix to HBM. Fewer memory reads and writes give faster, exact attention.

Advanced How would you extend a model's context window from 8k to 128k?

Rescale RoPE frequencies (position interpolation, NTK-aware scaling, or YaRN), then continue training on long documents. Ensure efficient attention kernels, and evaluate with long-context benchmarks (needle-in-a-haystack, multi-doc QA). Watch KV cache memory.

Training and scaling (16)

Basic What are the stages of training a chat LLM?

Pretraining on large text (next-token prediction) gives a base model. SFT on instruction/response demonstrations, then preference optimization (RLHF or DPO) and possibly RL on verifiable tasks, give a helpful, aligned assistant.

Basic What is the difference between a base model and an instruct model?

A base model continues text. An instruct (chat) model has been post-trained to follow instructions, use a chat template and refuse unsafe requests.

Basic What is perplexity?

exp of the average negative log-likelihood per token. Lower is better. It is the effective number of equally likely choices the model is uncertain between.

Basic What is teacher forcing?

During training the model is fed the true previous tokens instead of its own predictions, so all positions can be trained in parallel in one forward pass.

Medium Estimate the training FLOPs for a 7B model trained on 2T tokens.

C is about 6ND = 6 x 7e9 x 2e12, about 8.4e22 FLOPs (8.1e22 with the exact 6.74B). At about 125 TFLOP/s effective per A100 (40% MFU) that is roughly 180k GPU-hours.

Medium What did the Chinchilla paper find?

For a fixed compute budget, models should be trained on about 20 tokens per parameter. Many earlier models were too big and undertrained. Example: Chinchilla, 70B on 1.4T tokens.

Medium Why would anyone train a small model on far more tokens than compute-optimal?

Inference cost scales with model size. Overtraining a small model reaches the same quality at lower serving cost (Llama 3 8B used over 15T tokens), trading extra training compute for cheaper inference.

Medium Data parallelism vs tensor parallelism vs pipeline parallelism?

Data: split batches, all-reduce gradients. Tensor: split matrices within layers, with communication every layer (needs NVLink). Pipeline: split layers into stages, with micro-batches to reduce bubbles. Large runs combine all three.

Medium What are ZeRO stages 1, 2 and 3?

Sharding of the training state across data-parallel ranks. Stage 1 shards optimizer states, stage 2 adds gradients, stage 3 adds parameters (similar to FSDP full sharding). Memory per GPU drops roughly linearly with the number of GPUs.

Medium Why bf16 over fp16 for training?

bf16 keeps fp32's 8-bit exponent (same dynamic range), so gradients rarely underflow and loss scaling is unnecessary. fp16 has more mantissa but a narrow range, so it needs loss scaling.

Medium What is knowledge distillation?

Training a smaller student model to match a larger teacher's outputs (soft probabilities or generated responses). It transfers capability at lower inference cost.

Medium How is pass@k computed for code models?

Generate n samples per problem with c passing. The unbiased estimate is 1 - C(n-c, k)/C(n, k). With n = 10 and c = 3: pass@1 = 0.3, pass@5 is about 0.917.

Advanced What causes hallucinations, and how do you reduce them in a product?

Models are trained to produce plausible text, not verified truth. Gaps in knowledge and pressure to always answer cause fabrication. Reduce it with retrieval grounding and citations, abstention options, lower temperature, verification tools, constrained outputs, and faithfulness evals.

Advanced What is test-time compute scaling?

Improving accuracy by spending more inference compute per query: long chains of thought (reasoning models), self-consistency sampling, search with verifiers. It complements training-time scaling.

Advanced What is activation checkpointing and when do you use it?

Store only some activations in the forward pass and recompute the rest during backward. It cuts activation memory greatly at about one extra forward pass of compute. Use it when activations limit batch size or sequence length.

Advanced How would you detect benchmark contamination in a model?

Check n-gram or embedding overlap between benchmark items and training data, test on rephrased or freshly written items, compare performance on pre- vs post-cutoff items, and look for verbatim completion of test items.

Prompt engineering (11)

Basic What makes a good prompt?

A clear role and task, relevant context in delimiters, an explicit output format, constraints, examples for tricky cases, and a fallback instruction for when the answer is unknown.

Basic Zero-shot vs few-shot prompting?

Zero-shot gives only instructions. Few-shot adds a few input/output examples in the prompt (in-context learning), which helps with format and edge cases without changing weights.

Basic What is chain-of-thought prompting?

Asking the model to reason step by step before answering (or showing reasoning in examples). It gives the model intermediate computation, which helps multi-step problems.

Basic What is a system prompt?

Instructions with higher priority that set the model's role, rules, tone and format for the whole conversation, separate from user messages.

Medium What is self-consistency?

Sample multiple reasoning paths at non-zero temperature and take the majority final answer. With independent errors, accuracy improves (0.6 per sample gives 0.68 for majority-of-5). Costs scale with the number of samples.

Medium How do you get reliable JSON output from an LLM?

Use structured-output or JSON-schema mode (constrained decoding) or function calling. Validate with a parser, keep the schema simple, include an unknown option, and keep evals for value correctness.

Medium What is ReAct?

A prompting and agent pattern that interleaves reasoning (thoughts), actions (tool calls) and observations (tool results) until the task is solved. It is the basis of most tool-using agents.

Medium How do you iterate on prompts systematically?

Build an eval set and a baseline, change one thing at a time, measure on the same items with a paired comparison, version prompts in git, and log prompt versions in production.

Medium How does example order or selection affect few-shot prompting?

Models can be biased toward the most recent or most frequent labels in the examples. Use diverse, balanced, representative examples, and retrieve similar examples dynamically for large pools.

Advanced How would you defend a prompt against injection from retrieved documents?

Delimit untrusted content and tell the model to treat it as data, but rely on architecture: least-privilege tools, no actions triggered directly by retrieved text, human approval for risky actions, output filtering, and allowlisted links. Prompts alone are not a security boundary.

Advanced When would prompting not be enough, and what would you do?

When the task needs a consistent style or format at scale, domain behavior the model lacks, lower latency or cost, or knowledge that changes. Then add RAG for knowledge, fine-tune for behavior or distill for cost, measured against the prompt baseline.

Inference (18)

Basic What is the KV cache and why does it exist?

It stores each past token's key and value vectors for every layer, so a new token only computes its own Q, K, V and attends to the cached ones. Without it, every step would recompute all previous tokens.

Basic What is the difference between prefill and decode?

Prefill processes the whole prompt in one parallel forward pass (compute bound) and produces the first token. Decode generates one token per pass (memory-bandwidth bound).

Basic What is time to first token vs time per output token?

TTFT is the delay before the first token (queueing + prefill). TPOT, or inter-token latency, is the time between subsequent tokens (decode speed).

Basic How much memory do an 8B model's weights need in bf16?

8e9 x 2 bytes = 16 GB (14.9 GiB), before KV cache and overhead.

Basic What is quantization?

Storing weights (and sometimes activations or KV) in fewer bits, like int8 or int4 instead of bf16. It cuts memory and bandwidth at some accuracy cost.

Medium Write the KV cache size formula and compute it for 32 layers, 8 KV heads, head_dim 128, fp16, 4 x 8192 tokens.

2 x layers x kv_heads x head_dim x seq x batch x bytes = 2 x 32 x 8 x 128 x 8192 x 4 x 2 = 2^32 bytes = 4 GiB.

Medium Why is single-stream decoding memory-bandwidth bound?

Each token requires reading every weight once but does only about 2 FLOPs per weight, around 1 FLOP per byte for bf16, far below GPU ridge points (about 150 to 300 FLOP/byte). Time is roughly bytes read divided by bandwidth.

Medium Why does 4-bit weight-only quantization speed up decoding?

It reads about 4x fewer weight bytes per token. Kernels dequantize to 16-bit and multiply in 16-bit (W4A16), so the win is bandwidth, not int4 arithmetic and not fewer layers.

Medium What is continuous batching?

The server adds new requests and removes finished ones at every decode step instead of waiting for a whole batch to finish. This keeps GPU slots busy and greatly increases throughput.

Medium What problem does PagedAttention solve?

KV cache fragmentation and over-reservation. KV is stored in fixed-size blocks allocated on demand and mapped by a block table, so more sequences fit and prefixes can be shared.

Medium How does speculative decoding preserve output quality?

A draft model proposes k tokens. The target verifies them in one pass, accepting each with probability min(1, p/q) and resampling from max(0, p - q) on rejection. The output distribution exactly equals the target's.

Medium What is prompt or prefix caching and how should prompts be ordered to benefit?

Reuse of KV (or provider-side cache) for an identical prompt prefix. Order content from static (system prompt, tools) to semi-static (documents) to dynamic (user question), and avoid timestamps or IDs early in the prompt.

Medium Why does Tamil text use about 3x more tokens than English?

Tamil code points are 3 bytes in UTF-8, and byte-level BPE merges were learned mostly from English, so Tamil byte sequences stay fragmented into many tokens.

Advanced Explain the roofline model and use it to compare prefill and decode.

Attainable FLOP/s = min(peak compute, bandwidth x arithmetic intensity). Decode at batch 1 has intensity of about 1 FLOP/byte (memory bound). Prefill over N tokens reuses each weight N times (compute bound). Batching moves decode right on the roofline.

Advanced When can speculative decoding make things slower?

When the acceptance rate is low, the draft is expensive, or the server already runs large batches (compute bound), so verifying extra tokens competes for compute.

Advanced What does grammar-constrained decoding guarantee and not guarantee?

It guarantees outputs match the grammar or schema (barring truncation) by masking illegal tokens. It does not guarantee correct values, removes no need for evals, and can force wrong values with overly strict enums.

Advanced How would you size GPUs to serve a 70B model to 32 users at 8k context?

Weights: 140 GB in bf16 (35 GB in int4). KV: 320 KiB/token x 262,144 tokens = 80 GiB in fp16. So bf16 needs about 230 GB plus overhead (for example 4 x 80 GB with tensor parallelism). int4 weights + fp8 KV fit in about 80 to 120 GB.

Advanced Tensor parallelism vs pipeline parallelism for inference latency?

Tensor parallelism splits each layer across GPUs and lowers per-token latency but needs fast interconnect for per-layer all-reduces. Pipeline parallelism adds stage-to-stage latency and helps fit models across nodes, mainly for throughput.

Fine-tuning (14)

Basic What is fine-tuning?

Continuing training of a pretrained model on task-specific data so it adapts its behavior (format, style, domain), instead of training from scratch.

Basic What is LoRA in one sentence?

Freeze the pretrained weights and learn a low-rank update B x A (scaled by alpha/r) added to selected weight matrices, training only about 0.1 to 1% of the parameters.

Basic When should you fine-tune vs use RAG vs prompt?

Prompt first. Use RAG for knowledge that changes or needs citations. Fine-tune for consistent behavior or format, domain style, or cheaper and faster distilled models.

Basic What is SFT?

Supervised fine-tuning on (prompt, ideal response) pairs with next-token loss, usually computed only on the response tokens.

Medium Compute LoRA trainable parameters for r = 16 on q and v (4096 x 4096) over 32 layers.

Per module r(d_in + d_out) = 16 x 8192 = 131,072. Times 2 modules times 32 layers = 8,388,608.

Medium What memory does LoRA save and not save?

It saves gradients and optimizer states (and the fp32 master copy) for the frozen weights. It does not remove activation memory, because backprop still passes through the frozen layers.

Medium What is QLoRA?

LoRA on top of a frozen base stored in 4-bit NF4, with double quantization and paged optimizers. Adapters train in bf16. It lets 7B to 70B fine-tunes fit on far less GPU memory.

Medium Explain DPO and how it differs from RLHF with PPO.

DPO optimizes preferences directly with a logistic loss on the policy's log-probability ratios versus a frozen reference for chosen vs rejected responses. No reward model and no RL sampling loop. PPO-RLHF trains a reward model and runs online RL with a KL penalty.

Medium What is catastrophic forgetting and how do you mitigate it?

Losing general capabilities after narrow fine-tuning. Mitigate with PEFT or a lower learning rate, fewer epochs, mixing general replay data, and evaluating general benchmarks alongside the task.

Medium Why mask prompt tokens in the SFT loss?

So gradients come only from the response tokens. The model learns to answer rather than to reproduce prompts, and long shared system prompts do not dominate the loss.

Medium What is the role of alpha in LoRA?

The update is scaled by alpha/r, which keeps the effective update magnitude stable when changing rank. With r = 16 and alpha = 32 the scale is 2.

Advanced KTO, ORPO, GRPO: what problem does each address?

KTO learns from unpaired good/bad labels. ORPO folds preference alignment into SFT with an odds-ratio term and no reference model. GRPO is a PPO variant without a critic, using group-normalized rewards over several samples per prompt, popular for verifiable reasoning rewards.

Advanced How would you build a dataset for fine-tuning a support model?

Collect real conversations, clean and deduplicate them, remove PII, label or rewrite to the target style, balance intents, include refusals and edge cases, split by customer or time to avoid leakage, and hold out an eval set that mirrors production.

Advanced Can fine-tuning reliably inject new facts?

Not reliably. Facts seen few times are poorly memorized and fine-tuning on new knowledge can increase hallucination. Prefer RAG for facts, and fine-tune for behavior and format.

RAG (16)

Basic What is RAG and why use it?

Retrieval-augmented generation: retrieve relevant documents for a query and include them in the prompt, so the model answers from current, private or citable knowledge instead of only its weights.

Basic What are the main components of a RAG pipeline?

Ingestion (parse, chunk, embed, index), retrieval (vector and/or keyword search), optional reranking, prompt construction with context, generation, and evaluation and monitoring.

Basic What is a vector database?

A store with approximate nearest-neighbor indexes (HNSW, IVF) for fast similarity search over embeddings, usually with metadata filtering.

Basic Why chunk documents, and what size?

Embeddings and context windows work better with focused passages. Typical chunks are a few hundred tokens with some overlap, ideally split on structure (headings, paragraphs). Tune by retrieval evals.

Basic Cosine similarity vs dot product?

Cosine normalizes by vector length. Dot product does not. After L2 normalization they rank identically. Use the metric the embedding model was trained for.

Medium Your recall@20 is 0.93 but answer accuracy is 0.61 and wrong chunks get quoted. What do you do?

Retrieval is fine and ranking or generation is the problem. Add a cross-encoder reranker and pass only the top 3 to 5 chunks. Do not raise temperature, retrieve more, or swap embeddings.

Medium Bi-encoder vs cross-encoder?

Bi-encoders embed query and document separately, so documents are precomputed and search is fast (first stage). Cross-encoders read query and document together, which is more accurate but needs one forward pass per pair (reranking).

Medium Explain BM25 and why hybrid search helps.

BM25 scores term matches with idf weighting, saturating term frequency (k1) and length normalization (b). Hybrid search fuses BM25 (exact terms, codes, names) with dense retrieval (semantics), usually via RRF.

Medium What is Reciprocal Rank Fusion?

Score = sum over ranked lists of 1/(k + rank) with 1-based rank and k = 60. It needs no score normalization, and items found by several retrievers rise to the top.

Medium What happens if you upgrade the embedding model?

Vectors from different models are incompatible even at the same dimension, so re-embed the entire corpus and the queries. Do a blue/green migration with dual writes and evaluate before switching.

Medium How do you evaluate a RAG system?

Retrieval metrics (recall@k, MRR, nDCG) on labeled query-to-document pairs, plus generation metrics: answer correctness, faithfulness or groundedness, citation precision, abstention. Measure each stage separately to localize failures.

Medium What is 'lost in the middle'?

LLMs use information at the start and end of a long context better than in the middle. Put the most relevant chunks first or last, and pass fewer, better chunks.

Advanced How do you enforce document-level permissions in RAG?

Store ACL metadata with each chunk and pre-filter retrieval by the user's groups (filter-aware ANN). Never rely on the prompt to hide content. Test with cross-permission probes.

Advanced HNSW vs IVF-PQ: when would you choose each?

HNSW: high recall and low latency, but memory-heavy and slower to build. Good up to tens of millions of vectors in RAM. IVF-PQ: compressed and memory-efficient for very large corpora, with lower recall, reranked with full vectors.

Advanced How would you handle multi-hop questions in RAG?

Query decomposition into sub-questions, iterative retrieval (retrieve, read, retrieve again), agentic RAG with a search tool, or graph-based retrieval over linked entities. Evaluate with multi-hop test sets.

Advanced What is the difference between post-filtering and pre-filtering in vector search?

Post-filtering runs ANN then applies metadata filters, which can return fewer than k results for selective filters. Pre-filtering restricts the candidate set first, which is correct but needs index support (filtered HNSW or partitioned indexes).

Evaluation (12)

Basic What is a golden set?

A curated, versioned set of representative inputs with expected outputs or rubrics, used to evaluate and regression-test a system.

Basic What is an LLM-as-judge?

Using an LLM to grade outputs against a rubric or compare two outputs. It is fast and cheap, but must be validated against human labels and checked for biases.

Basic Name classic text-generation metrics and their limitations.

Exact match, token F1, BLEU, ROUGE and BERTScore. They measure surface or embedding overlap with references and correlate poorly with quality on open-ended answers that can be phrased many ways.

Medium Prompt B scores 81% and A 78% on the same 200 questions, with 30 disagreements (18 for B). Is B better?

Not significantly. Use a paired test: the exact sign test on the 30 discordant pairs gives p = 0.36. A 3-point gap with 30 disagreements is within chance.

Medium Why use a paired test instead of comparing two proportions?

Both systems answered the same items, so item difficulty is shared. Paired tests (McNemar, sign test) use only discordant items, which is correct and usually more powerful than the unpaired z-test.

Medium What biases do LLM judges have and how do you mitigate them?

Position bias (swap the order and keep only consistent wins), verbosity bias (rubrics penalizing padding), self-preference (a judge from a different model family), and score compression (use binary or pairwise criteria).

Medium How do you put a confidence interval on accuracy?

Wilson interval for a proportion, or bootstrap (resample items with replacement). For two systems on the same items, use a paired bootstrap of the difference.

Medium What is eval contamination?

Test items appearing in training data, which inflates scores. Defend with private held-out sets, near-duplicate checks between train and eval, and fresh items.

Advanced How many eval questions do you need to detect a 3-point improvement?

It depends on the discordance rate. With 15% discordant and a true 60/40 split of disagreements, about 194 discordant pairs are needed for 80% power, so about 1,300 questions. Unpaired designs need about 2,800 per arm.

Advanced What is the multiple comparisons problem in prompt experiments?

Testing many variants inflates false positives: 20 tests at alpha 0.05 gives a 64% chance of at least one false winner. Use Bonferroni, Holm or Benjamini-Hochberg corrections, or confirm on a fresh test set.

Advanced Offline vs online evaluation: how do they fit together?

Offline (golden sets, component metrics) gates releases quickly and repeatably. Online (A/B tests, user feedback, task success) measures real impact. Production failures feed back into the offline set.

Advanced How would you evaluate an agent?

Task success rate in simulated or sandboxed environments, tool-call accuracy, policy compliance, step and cost efficiency, and trajectory review. Plus regression suites and online containment and escalation metrics.

Agents (13)

Basic What is an LLM agent?

A system where an LLM decides which actions (tool calls) to take in a loop, observing results, until a goal is met. Typically an LLM plus tools plus memory or state plus a control loop with limits.

Basic Workflow vs agent?

A workflow follows predefined steps (prompt chains, routing). An agent chooses its own steps dynamically. Prefer workflows when steps are known: more predictable and easier to test.

Basic What is function or tool calling?

The model outputs a structured call (function name + JSON arguments) per provided schemas. Your code executes it and returns the result for the next turn.

Basic Why do agents need step limits?

To prevent infinite loops, runaway cost and latency. Cap steps, tool calls, tokens and wall-clock time, and detect repeated identical calls.

Medium A create_refund call times out after the refund was created. What is the safest design?

Generate an idempotency key once per logical refund, persist it, and reuse it on every retry, so the server deduplicates. Timeouts are unknown outcomes. Backoff alone or LLM-decided retries are unsafe.

Medium At-least-once vs exactly-once?

At-least-once never loses a message but may duplicate it (retries). Exactly-once delivery is impractical over unreliable networks. You get an exactly-once effect with at-least-once delivery plus idempotent processing.

Medium Why add jitter to exponential backoff?

Without jitter, clients that failed together retry together (thundering herd). Full jitter, uniform(0, min(cap, base x 2^n)), spreads retries out.

Medium What is prompt injection, and how do you defend an agent?

Instructions hidden in user input or retrieved content that hijack the model. Defend with least-privilege tools, permission checks in code, human approval for risky actions, separation of untrusted content, output filtering and audit logs. Prompts alone are insufficient.

Medium How do you manage agent memory?

Short-term: the conversation and scratchpad, trimmed or summarized to a token budget. Long-term: a store of facts, preferences or episodes retrieved by relevance. Checkpoint state so runs can resume.

Medium Compare LangChain, LangGraph and CrewAI.

LangChain provides building blocks (models, prompts, retrievers, tools). LangGraph models agents as explicit state graphs with checkpointing, cycles and human-in-the-loop, good for controllable production flows. CrewAI offers role-based multi-agent crews with tasks, quick for collaborative agent patterns.

Advanced What are the outbox and saga patterns and when would an agent need them?

Outbox: write the state change and a message in one DB transaction, then a relay publishes it, so no lost or phantom events. Saga: a multi-step process with compensating actions on failure. Agents performing multi-service side effects (refund + notify + update CRM) need them.

Advanced What is MCP?

The Model Context Protocol standardizes how apps expose tools, resources and prompts to LLM clients over JSON-RPC (stdio or HTTP transports). Build an integration once and use it from any MCP client. Tool outputs remain untrusted input.

Advanced How do you make a multi-agent system reliable?

Clear roles and contracts, structured messages, a supervisor with explicit termination conditions, shared state with checkpoints, per-agent tool scopes, budgets, tracing across agents, and end-to-end evals. Start with the fewest agents that work.

Decoding (11)

Basic What does temperature do?

It divides logits before the softmax. Lower T sharpens the distribution (more deterministic), higher T flattens it (more diverse). T = 0 is treated as greedy. It never changes which token is most likely.

Basic Greedy decoding vs sampling?

Greedy takes the argmax each step (deterministic, can be repetitive). Sampling draws from the distribution (diverse), usually with temperature, top-k or top-p.

Basic What is top-p (nucleus) sampling?

Keep the smallest set of most-probable tokens whose cumulative probability reaches p, renormalize, then sample. It adapts the candidate count to the model's confidence.

Medium Top-k vs top-p: which adapts better and why?

Top-p, because the number of kept tokens grows when the distribution is flat and shrinks when it is peaked. Top-k keeps a fixed count regardless.

Medium What is beam search and why is it not guaranteed optimal?

It keeps the best B partial sequences at each step and extends them all. Pruning by prefix score can discard prefixes whose continuations would score best, so it can miss the global optimum.

Medium Why do beam search outputs favor short sequences, and how is it fixed?

Summing negative log-probs penalizes every extra token. Length normalization (score / length^alpha, or the GNMT penalty) corrects it.

Medium What are logprobs useful for?

Confidence estimation, classification scores, detecting low-confidence spans, comparing candidate answers, and computing perplexity. They are not calibrated truth probabilities.

Medium What is min-p sampling?

Keep tokens with probability at least min_p x p_max, then renormalize. The cutoff scales with the top token's confidence.

Advanced Why might temperature 0 still give non-deterministic outputs?

Floating-point non-associativity in batched GPU kernels (different batch compositions or kernel choices) can flip near-ties in the argmax. Providers generally do not guarantee bit-identical outputs.

Advanced When would you use Viterbi instead of beam search?

When scores factor over adjacent tokens (a first-order Markov chain, like HMMs or CRFs), Viterbi dynamic programming finds the exact best sequence in O(T x V^2). LLMs condition on full prefixes, so beam or sampling is used there.

Advanced How do repetition, frequency and presence penalties differ?

The CTRL-style repetition penalty divides positive logits (multiplies negative ones) of seen tokens by r. Frequency penalty subtracts proportional to the count. Presence penalty subtracts a flat amount once a token has appeared.

System design (12)

Basic What are the key latency metrics for an LLM service?

Time to first token, time per output token (inter-token latency), total latency at p50/p95/p99, and throughput in tokens/s and requests/s.

Basic Hosted API vs self-hosted model?

APIs: fast to start, top quality, pay per token, less control. Self-hosted: data control, customization, cost advantages at high steady volume, but GPU ops burden.

Basic Why stream LLM responses?

Users see output after the time to first token instead of waiting for the full response, which improves perceived latency, and they can stop early.

Medium How would you estimate the cost of an LLM feature?

Requests per day x (input tokens x input price + output tokens x output price), minus cache discounts. For self-hosting: peak tokens/s divided by per-GPU throughput gives the GPU count, then add redundancy.

Medium What is a model cascade?

Route easy requests to a cheap model and escalate hard or low-confidence ones to a stronger model. With 80% handled at cost 1 and 20% at cost 10, average cost is 2.8 instead of 10.

Medium What should you monitor in an LLM application?

Latency, errors, throughput, token usage and cost, cache hit rate, quality signals (judge samples, user feedback, groundedness), retrieval health, safety triggers, and drift in inputs.

Medium How do you roll out a new prompt or model safely?

Offline golden-set evals with paired tests, then shadow traffic, then a canary to a small slice, then a gradual ramp with guardrail metrics and instant rollback through versioned configs.

Medium Semantic caching: benefits and risks?

It saves cost and latency on near-duplicate queries. The risks are wrong answers for similar but different questions, stale content and leaking personalized answers. Use similarity thresholds, TTLs and per-user or tenant scoping.

Advanced Design a document Q&A assistant for 2M internal documents with permissions.

Event-driven ingestion (parse, structure-aware chunks, embeddings, vector + BM25 indexes with ACL metadata), hybrid retrieval pre-filtered by ACL, cross-encoder rerank to the top 5, a grounded prompt with citations and an abstain path, streaming, golden-set evals (recall@k, faithfulness, citation precision), and monitoring of retrieval gaps and permissions.

Advanced Design a support agent that can issue refunds.

Intent routing to deterministic workflows, plus a bounded agent for the long tail. Tools with server-side ownership and amount checks, idempotency keys, human approval above thresholds, checkpointed state, outbox notifications, simulated-conversation evals, and a shadow-then-canary rollout.

Advanced How would you serve a model with spiky traffic cheaply?

Autoscale on queue depth and KV utilization with warm pools, continuous batching, quantization, prefix caching, a cascade to smaller models, burst overflow to an API provider, request prioritization, and graceful degradation.

Advanced How do you use Little's law in LLM capacity planning?

Concurrency = arrival rate x time in system. At 35 req/s and 10 s each there are 350 concurrent sequences. Multiply by tokens per sequence and KV bytes per token to size KV memory (about 83 GB for Llama-3-8B at 1,800 tokens).

Python and engineering (9)

Basic What is the time complexity of lookups in a Python dict and list?

dict and set membership is O(1) average (hash table). List membership is O(n). Use sets or dicts for frequent membership tests.

Basic What is the difference between a list and a generator?

A list materializes all items in memory. A generator yields items lazily one at a time, which is memory-efficient for streaming large data.

Basic How do you sort by multiple keys with tie-breaking in Python?

Use a key tuple: sorted(items, key=lambda i: (-score[i], i)) sorts by score descending, then index ascending. Python's sort is stable.

Medium What is the GIL and how does it affect AI workloads?

CPython's Global Interpreter Lock lets one thread execute Python bytecode at a time. CPU-bound Python code does not speed up with threads (use processes or native libraries). I/O-bound work (API calls) benefits from threads or asyncio, and numpy and PyTorch release the GIL in native code.

Medium When would you use asyncio for LLM applications?

For many concurrent I/O-bound calls (LLM APIs, retrieval, tools): asyncio.gather with a semaphore for rate limits gives high concurrency with low overhead. Not for CPU-bound work.

Medium Why can floating-point sums differ between runs, and when does it matter?

Addition is not associative, so a different summation order can change the last bits. It matters for exact tie-breaking and reproducibility. Fix the order, or use exact arithmetic where possible.

Medium How would you implement an LRU cache?

An ordered dict (or a hash map plus a doubly linked list): move a key to the end on access, evict from the front when over capacity. O(1) get and put. In Python: collections.OrderedDict or functools.lru_cache.

Advanced How do you test non-deterministic LLM code?

Unit-test deterministic parts (parsers, tools, retrieval) normally. Mock LLM calls for logic. Use eval suites with thresholds for model behavior, fixed seeds or temperature 0 where possible, and property-based checks (valid JSON, schema compliance).

Advanced How would you make a streaming JSON tool-call parser robust?

Track brace depth, in-string state and escape state across chunks (a state machine), emit objects only when depth returns to 0, keep O(current object) memory, and validate with json.loads before executing anything.

Behavioral and projects (7)

Basic Walk me through an LLM project you built.

Use a structure: problem and users, constraints, architecture (models, retrieval, tools), what you measured (eval set, metrics), key iterations and their measured impact, failures and fixes, and results. Mention numbers (latency, accuracy, cost).

Basic Why do you want to work on AI/ML engineering?

Connect your genuine interest to concrete things you built (for example agents with LangGraph or CrewAI, RAG pipelines), what you learned, and the impact you want, tailored to the company's product.

Medium Tell me about a time an AI feature did not work as expected.

STAR: the situation, the task, the action (how you diagnosed it: stage-level metrics, traces, error analysis), the fix, and the measured result, plus what you changed in your process (for example adding regression evals).

Medium How do you decide between building with an API vs open-source models?

Weigh quality requirements, data privacy, volume and cost curves, latency, customization needs and team ops capacity. Prototype with an API, measure, then revisit self-hosting at scale.

Medium How do you keep up with a fast-moving field?

Read primary sources (papers, model cards, release notes), reproduce key results on small experiments, maintain your own eval sets to test claims, and follow a few high-signal practitioners.

Advanced How would you explain a model's limitations to a non-technical stakeholder?

Use concrete examples of failure modes, give measured error rates with uncertainty, state what safeguards exist (human review, citations), and frame the decision as risk vs benefit with a monitoring plan.

Advanced Describe how you would set up evaluation for a brand-new LLM feature.

Define success with stakeholders, collect representative and edge-case inputs, label references or rubrics, choose component and end-to-end metrics, validate any LLM judge against humans, run baselines, use paired tests for changes, and wire it into CI and production monitoring.

Classical ML (14)

Basic Linear regression: what does it minimise and how is it solved?

The sum or mean of squared errors. Solve in closed form with the normal equation (X^T X)^(-1) X^T y, or by gradient descent for large data and other models.

Basic Why regularise a model, and what do L1 and L2 do?

To limit overfitting. L2 (ridge) shrinks weights smoothly; L1 (lasso) drives some weights to exactly zero, performing feature selection.

Basic How does logistic regression produce a probability?

A linear score is passed through the sigmoid; it is trained by minimising log loss, whose gradient is (p - y) x.

Basic Explain k-nearest neighbours and its main weaknesses.

Predict by a vote or mean of the k closest training points. It needs scaled features, is slow at prediction time and degrades in high dimensions.

Medium Bagging versus boosting?

Bagging trains models independently on bootstrap samples and averages them to reduce variance. Boosting trains models sequentially on the errors of the previous ones to reduce bias.

Medium What makes a random forest different from bagged trees?

It also samples a random subset of features at each split, decorrelating the trees so averaging reduces variance more.

Medium How does gradient boosting work?

Each new small tree fits the negative gradient (residuals for squared error) of the current ensemble, added with a learning rate. XGBoost, LightGBM and CatBoost add regularisation and speedups.

Medium How do decision trees choose splits?

By the largest impurity reduction (Gini or entropy information gain) over candidate features and thresholds.

Medium What is the kernel trick?

Replace dot products with a kernel function, such as RBF, so a linear model works in an implicit high-dimensional space without computing it.

Medium What assumptions does naive Bayes make, and why does it still work?

Features are conditionally independent given the class. The assumption is false, but the ranking of classes is often right, especially for text, and the model is fast and robust with little data.

Advanced Explain the bias-variance trade-off.

Expected error is bias squared plus variance plus irreducible noise. Simple models underfit (bias), flexible models overfit (variance). Choose complexity by validation, and use regularisation, ensembles and more data to manage it.

Advanced When does k-means fail and what are alternatives?

It assumes round, similar-size clusters, depends on initialisation and needs k. Use k-means++, Gaussian mixtures, DBSCAN for arbitrary shapes and noise, or hierarchical clustering.

Advanced How does PCA work and what are its limits?

It projects onto the orthogonal directions of maximum variance (covariance eigenvectors). It is linear, unsupervised, sensitive to scaling and may discard low-variance but predictive directions.

Advanced Why might a tuned gradient boosting model beat a neural network on tabular data?

Tabular features are heterogeneous with irregular interactions and modest sample sizes. Trees handle mixed types, missing values and non-smooth functions without heavy tuning.

Math for ML (13)

Basic What is a dot product and what does it measure?

The sum of element-wise products; it measures alignment, large and positive when vectors point the same way, zero when perpendicular.

Basic What is a gradient?

The vector of partial derivatives of a function; it points in the direction of steepest increase, so gradient descent moves against it.

Basic State Bayes' rule.

P(H | D) = P(D | H) P(H) / P(D): the posterior equals likelihood times prior divided by evidence.

Basic What is the chain rule and why is it central to deep learning?

The derivative of a composition is the product of derivatives. Backpropagation applies it layer by layer from the loss back to each weight.

Medium Why does a rare disease test with high accuracy often give a low positive predictive value?

Base rates dominate: with 1 percent prevalence, 95 percent sensitivity and 90 percent specificity, false positives from the large healthy group outnumber true positives, giving about 9 percent.

Medium What are eigenvalues and eigenvectors and where do they appear in ML?

A v = lambda v: directions only scaled by the matrix. Covariance eigenvectors are principal components; eigenvalues are variances along them. Also used in spectral methods and stability analysis.

Medium Entropy, cross-entropy and KL divergence?

Entropy is average surprise; cross-entropy is the cost of coding p with a code for q; KL(p||q) equals cross-entropy minus entropy, is non-negative and asymmetric.

Medium Why does MLE with Gaussian noise give squared error?

The Gaussian log likelihood is a constant minus the squared residual over twice the variance, so maximising it minimises squared error.

Medium What does a Gaussian prior on weights correspond to?

L2 regularisation (MAP estimation); a Laplace prior corresponds to L1.

Advanced Why is the SVD important?

Every matrix factors as U S V^T; truncating to the largest singular values gives the best low-rank approximation, used in compression, PCA, recommender systems and LoRA.

Advanced Compare SGD, momentum, RMSProp and Adam.

SGD follows noisy gradients; momentum averages gradients to smooth them; RMSProp scales steps by running squared gradients; Adam combines both with bias correction.

Advanced What is convexity and why does it matter?

A convex loss has a single global minimum, so gradient descent converges to it. Neural network losses are non-convex, yet SGD still finds good solutions in practice.

Advanced Why subtract the maximum before computing softmax?

It prevents overflow in exp without changing the result, since softmax is invariant to adding a constant to all logits.

Deep learning (13)

Basic Explain backpropagation.

A forward pass computes the loss while storing intermediate values; a backward pass applies the chain rule from the loss to every parameter, accumulating gradients where values are reused.

Basic Why do we need non-linear activation functions?

Without them a stack of linear layers collapses to a single linear map and cannot model complex functions.

Basic What is overfitting and how do you reduce it?

Fitting noise: low training error but high validation error. Use more data, augmentation, dropout, weight decay, early stopping, smaller models.

Medium What causes vanishing and exploding gradients and how are they handled?

Products of many small or large factors through depth. Use ReLU-family activations, good initialisation (He, Xavier), normalisation, residual connections, gradient clipping and gated units (LSTM).

Medium BatchNorm versus LayerNorm?

BatchNorm normalises over the batch per feature and depends on batch statistics; LayerNorm normalises over the features of each example, suiting transformers and variable batch sizes.

Medium How does dropout work at training and test time?

It randomly zeroes activations with probability p during training, scaling the rest by 1/(1-p); at test time it is off, so the network acts like an ensemble average.

Medium Why is the learning rate the most important hyperparameter?

Too high diverges or oscillates, too low is slow or stuck. Use warm-up and decay schedules, and tune it first.

Medium What is weight initialisation and why does it matter?

Starting weights set the scale of activations and gradients. Xavier (tanh) and He (ReLU) scale variance by layer width to keep signals stable; all-zero init breaks symmetry.

Medium What do convolutions provide over fully connected layers?

Weight sharing, locality and translation equivariance, giving far fewer parameters and better generalisation for grid-like data.

Advanced Why do residual connections help?

They give gradients a direct path, so very deep networks train; each block learns a correction to the identity.

Advanced How would you debug a network that does not train?

Overfit a tiny batch, check the initial loss equals ln K, verify data and labels, check the learning rate and gradient norms, test with and without regularisation, and change one thing at a time.

Advanced AdamW versus Adam with L2 regularisation?

In Adam, L2 added to the gradient is scaled by the adaptive step, weakening regularisation. AdamW decouples weight decay from the gradient update, which generalises better.

Advanced Why did transformers replace RNNs?

Attention connects any two positions directly, avoiding long gradient paths, and processes sequences in parallel on GPUs, scaling to far more data.

NLP and embeddings (12)

Basic What is tokenisation and why subwords?

Splitting text into units the model processes. Subwords balance vocabulary size and sequence length and handle unseen words by composing known pieces.

Basic TF-IDF in one sentence?

A term's weight is its frequency in the document times the log of how rare it is across documents, so distinctive words score high.

Basic What is an embedding?

A dense learned vector for a token or item such that similar things are close together.

Medium Describe how BPE is trained.

Start from characters, repeatedly merge the most frequent adjacent pair into a new symbol, and record the merges; encoding applies the merges in order.

Medium How does word2vec learn?

Skip-gram predicts context words from a centre word, using negative sampling to push real pairs together and random pairs apart; words sharing contexts end up with similar vectors.

Medium Static versus contextual embeddings?

Static embeddings give one vector per word type; contextual embeddings (BERT, GPT hidden states) give different vectors depending on the surrounding words.

Medium How are text embedding models trained?

Contrastively: matching pairs (question and answer, query and document) are pulled together and in-batch or hard negatives pushed apart using an InfoNCE loss.

Medium Why normalise embeddings?

Unit-length vectors make cosine similarity equal the dot product and remove length effects, simplifying and speeding search.

Advanced Explain IVF, PQ and HNSW.

IVF clusters vectors and searches the nearest clusters; PQ compresses vectors into short codes; HNSW is a layered neighbour graph searched greedily, giving the best recall-latency trade-off at higher memory.

Advanced How do you evaluate an ANN index?

Recall@k against exact search at a given latency and memory, tuning parameters like nprobe or efSearch to a target recall, and testing under metadata filters.

Advanced Why can LLMs fail at character-level tasks?

They process subword tokens rather than characters, so letter-level structure is hidden inside tokens.

Advanced How would you choose an embedding model?

Shortlist by benchmark (MTEB) and language, test recall@k on your own labelled queries, consider dimension, cost, latency and max length, and plan for re-embedding when you change models.

LLM architectures (13)

Basic Write scaled dot-product attention and explain the scaling.

softmax(Q K^T / sqrt(d_k)) V. Dividing by sqrt(d_k) keeps score variance near 1 so the softmax does not saturate.

Basic What is the KV cache and why does it exist?

Keys and values of past tokens are stored so each new token only computes its own query, key and value, making generation linear per token instead of recomputing the prefix.

Medium MHA, MQA and GQA?

They differ in the number of K and V heads: one per query head, one total, or one per group. GQA keeps near-MHA quality with a much smaller KV cache.

Medium How does FlashAttention speed up attention?

It tiles Q, K and V in on-chip memory and uses an online softmax so the n x n score matrix is never written to main memory, reducing memory traffic while staying exact.

Medium What is RoPE?

Rotary position embedding rotates query and key pairs by position-dependent angles so their dot product depends on the relative distance between tokens.

Medium How does a mixture of experts layer work?

A router selects the top k of E expert MLPs per token; only those run, so total parameters grow while compute per token stays small.

Medium What are the costs of MoE?

All experts must be stored, routing needs load balancing, communication between devices is heavy, and fine-tuning is trickier.

Medium What does quantisation buy and what does it risk?

Lower memory and bandwidth (and speed for memory-bound decoding) by using fewer bits per weight. It can reduce quality, especially below 4 bits or with outliers, so evaluate after quantising.

Advanced GPTQ versus AWQ versus bitsandbytes NF4?

GPTQ corrects rounding error layer by layer with second-order information; AWQ protects salient weights identified from activations; NF4 is a 4-bit data type used to hold a frozen base model during QLoRA training.

Advanced How can a model handle contexts longer than it was trained on?

Scale RoPE positions (interpolation, NTK-aware, YaRN) and fine-tune on long sequences, plus efficient attention, KV compression and retrieval.

Advanced State space models versus transformers?

SSMs like Mamba process sequences with a recurrence of fixed-size state in linear time and constant memory per step, but recall arbitrary earlier tokens less precisely; hybrids combine both.

Advanced Estimate parameters of a decoder-only transformer.

About 12 d^2 per layer (4 d^2 attention, 8 d^2 MLP) times layers, plus embeddings; with GQA and gated MLPs adjust the attention and MLP terms.

Advanced Why does decoding tend to be memory-bandwidth bound?

Each generated token must read all weights and the KV cache from memory for little arithmetic, so speed is set by bytes moved, which quantisation, GQA and batching improve.

Alignment and safety (13)

Basic What are the stages after pretraining?

Supervised fine-tuning on instructions, preference optimisation (RLHF or DPO), safety training and red teaming, sometimes RL with verifiable rewards for reasoning.

Basic What is a reward model?

A model trained on human or AI preference comparisons to output a scalar score for a response, used to optimise a policy.

Basic What is prompt injection?

Untrusted text in the model's input that contains instructions the model follows, hijacking its behaviour, for example from a web page an agent reads.

Medium State the Bradley-Terry loss for reward models.

-log sigmoid(r(x, y_w) - r(x, y_l)); the preferred response should score higher than the rejected one.

Medium What does the KL term in RLHF do?

It penalises divergence from the reference model so the policy cannot drift into outputs that merely fool the reward model.

Medium How does DPO differ from RLHF with PPO?

DPO writes the preference objective directly in terms of policy and reference log-probabilities, with no reward model or RL loop; it is simpler and stable but offline.

Medium What is reward hacking?

The policy exploits flaws in the reward model or metric to score high without being better, for example by writing longer, flattering answers.

Medium How do you mitigate hallucination?

Retrieval with citations, abstention training and prompts, tool use for facts, self-consistency and verification, and evaluations that reward calibrated uncertainty.

Advanced What is GRPO and why use it?

Group relative policy optimisation samples several answers per prompt and uses the group's mean reward as the baseline, removing the need for a value network, which is cheaper than PPO.

Advanced How are reasoning models trained?

Reinforcement learning with verifiable rewards (correct final answers, passing tests) on long chains of thought, sometimes with process rewards; the model discovers reasoning strategies.

Advanced Why are prompt-only defences against injection insufficient?

Models cannot reliably separate instructions from data. Robust defence needs least-privilege tools, approval for risky actions, isolation of untrusted content and monitoring.

Advanced How would you red-team a model?

Combine human experts and automated adversaries across harm categories and attack types (role-play, encoding, multi-turn, indirect injection), measure attack success on held-out attacks, fix and retest.

Advanced What is sycophancy and how is it measured or reduced?

The model tells users what they want to hear. Measure with challenge prompts where the user is wrong; reduce by preference data that rewards honesty and by evaluation against flattery.

MLOps (12)

Basic What is training-serving skew?

Features computed differently in training and production, causing the model to see different data online; prevented by sharing feature definitions in a feature store.

Basic What is data leakage?

Information unavailable at prediction time reaching the training set, such as future-derived features or duplicates across splits, inflating offline metrics.

Basic What do you monitor for a deployed model?

System health (latency, errors), data quality and drift, prediction distributions, outcome metrics when labels arrive, and fairness slices.

Medium Data drift versus concept drift?

Data drift changes the input distribution; concept drift changes the relationship between inputs and the target. Concept drift needs labels or outcome metrics to detect.

Medium What is PSI and how do you read it?

The population stability index compares binned distributions: below 0.1 stable, 0.1 to 0.25 moderate shift, above 0.25 major shift.

Medium Shadow, canary and A/B deployment?

Shadow logs predictions without using them; canary serves a small share with monitoring; A/B randomises users to measure the causal impact of the new model.

Medium What does a feature store provide?

Single feature definitions, an offline store with point-in-time correct joins for training, an online store for low-latency serving, and reuse.

Medium How do you make ML experiments reproducible?

Version code, data, config and environment, fix seeds, record runs in a tracker such as MLflow, and store models in a registry with lineage.

Advanced How do you decide when to retrain?

On a schedule, on drift or performance triggers, or continuously; always evaluate the retrained model against production on a frozen set before promoting.

Advanced How would you handle delayed labels in monitoring?

Monitor proxy signals (drift, prediction distributions) immediately, join labels when they arrive to compute true metrics, and use holdout or randomised exploration traffic for unbiased evaluation.

Advanced What belongs in a pre-release model evaluation?

Comparison with production and a baseline on a frozen set, slice metrics, calibration, latency and memory, fairness checks, and behavioural tests such as invariance and directional expectations.

Advanced How do you reduce ML serving cost?

Batching, quantisation, distillation, caching predictions, right-sizing hardware, autoscaling, and routing easy requests to cheaper models.

Vision and multimodal (12)

Basic What are IoU and NMS?

IoU is the overlap area over union area of two boxes; non-maximum suppression keeps the best box and removes other boxes overlapping it above a threshold.

Basic Why use transfer learning in vision?

Pretrained backbones already encode general visual features, so fine-tuning needs less data and training time and usually performs better than training from scratch.

Basic What is data augmentation for images?

Random label-preserving transformations (crops, flips, colour jitter, mixup) that teach invariances and reduce overfitting.

Medium How does a vision transformer process an image?

It splits it into patches, embeds each as a token with position embeddings and a CLS token, and applies transformer encoder layers.

Medium CNN versus ViT trade-offs?

CNNs have locality and translation equivariance and work with less data; ViTs have little built-in bias, need more data or strong pretraining, and scale very well with global attention.

Medium What is mAP?

Mean average precision: the area under each class's precision-recall curve, averaged over classes (and over IoU thresholds in COCO).

Medium Semantic versus instance segmentation?

Semantic assigns a class to each pixel; instance also distinguishes separate objects of the same class.

Medium How does CLIP work?

Image and text encoders are trained contrastively on image-caption pairs so matching pairs are close in a shared space, enabling zero-shot classification and retrieval.

Advanced How is a vision-language model built?

A vision encoder produces patch features, a projector maps them into the LLM's embedding space as image tokens, and the LLM generates text, trained first on captions then on visual instructions.

Advanced Why do VLMs hallucinate objects and how do you reduce it?

Language priors override weak visual evidence. Use higher resolution, grounded training data, preference tuning against hallucination and tools such as OCR and detectors.

Advanced When use Dice loss?

For segmentation with a small foreground, where pixel accuracy is dominated by background; Dice (or focal loss) emphasises overlap of the rare class.

Advanced What is the receptive field and how do you increase it?

The input region affecting a unit. It grows with depth, kernel size, strides and dilated convolutions, or by attention.

Recommender systems (12)

Basic Collaborative versus content-based filtering?

Collaborative filtering uses behaviour of similar users or items; content-based matches item attributes to a user profile and works for new items.

Basic What is the cold-start problem?

New users or items lack interaction data. Use popularity, content features, onboarding questions and exploration.

Medium Explain matrix factorisation.

Users and items get latent vectors whose dot product predicts the rating; learn them by minimising error on observed entries with regularisation using SGD or ALS.

Medium Why a two-stage architecture?

Retrieval narrows millions of items to hundreds quickly with high recall; a heavier ranker scores those, and re-ranking applies diversity and business rules.

Medium What is a two-tower model?

Separate user and item networks produce vectors scored by a dot product; item vectors are precomputed and searched with ANN.

Medium Which metrics evaluate recommenders?

Precision and recall at k, MAP, MRR, nDCG, hit rate, AUC and log loss, plus coverage, diversity and calibration, confirmed by online A/B tests.

Medium What is position bias and how do you handle it?

Items at the top get more clicks regardless of relevance. Model position as a feature during training and fix it at serving, or use interleaving and propensity weighting.

Advanced How do bandits help recommenders?

They balance exploration and exploitation (epsilon-greedy, UCB, Thompson sampling), learning item quality faster and reducing regret; contextual bandits generalise across users and items.

Advanced What is a feedback loop in recommendation?

The system's exposure shapes future training data, reinforcing popular items and narrow tastes; counter with exploration, debiasing, diversity and randomised traffic.

Advanced How do you evaluate offline given biased logs?

Split by time, use inverse propensity scoring or doubly robust estimators, log exploration traffic, and validate with A/B tests.

Advanced How do you optimise for long-term value rather than clicks?

Use multi-task models with satisfaction, retention and quality signals, add counter-clickbait constraints, and measure long-horizon outcomes in experiments with holdouts.

Advanced Explicit versus implicit feedback losses?

Explicit ratings use squared error on observed entries. Implicit data uses ranking losses such as BPR, weighted ALS with confidence or sampled softmax because missing entries are not negatives.

Reinforcement learning (13)

Basic What is a Markov decision process?

States, actions, transition probabilities, rewards and a discount factor, with the next state depending only on the current state and action.

Basic What is the discount factor?

A number between 0 and 1 that weights future rewards; low values make the agent short-sighted, high values far-sighted.

Basic Exploration versus exploitation?

Exploitation uses the best known action; exploration tries others to learn. Methods include epsilon-greedy, UCB, Thompson sampling and entropy bonuses.

Medium State the Bellman equation.

The value of a state-action equals the immediate reward plus the discounted expected value of the best next state-action.

Medium Q-learning versus SARSA?

Both are TD methods; Q-learning is off-policy (target uses the max over next actions), SARSA is on-policy (target uses the action actually taken), making it more conservative near hazards.

Medium Why does DQN use experience replay and a target network?

Replay breaks correlations between consecutive samples and reuses data; the target network keeps the bootstrap target stable.

Medium What is a policy gradient and why use a baseline?

It increases the probability of actions in proportion to the return. A baseline reduces variance without biasing the gradient.

Medium Actor-critic methods?

An actor updates the policy using a critic's value estimates as the baseline or advantage, combining low variance with direct policy optimisation.

Advanced What is the deadly triad?

Function approximation, bootstrapping and off-policy training together can make value learning diverge; mitigated by replay, target networks and careful design.

Advanced Why is PPO popular?

Its clipped objective limits how far each update moves the policy, giving stable and simple on-policy training; it is used in robotics, games and RLHF.

Advanced How does RLHF map onto RL?

The prompt plus generated tokens is the state, the next token is the action, a reward model or verifier gives a terminal reward, and a KL penalty keeps the policy near the reference.

Advanced What is reward hacking and how do you reduce it?

Optimising a flawed reward without achieving the intent. Improve reward design, add constraints and penalties, use human feedback, adversarial testing and monitor behaviour.

Advanced What is offline RL?

Learning a policy from a fixed dataset without interacting; the challenge is avoiding actions poorly covered by the data, handled with conservative value estimates or policy constraints.

Statistics and experiments (13)

Basic What is a p-value?

The probability of data at least as extreme as observed if the null hypothesis were true; it is not the probability that the null is true.

Basic What is a confidence interval?

A range produced by a procedure that covers the true value in a stated fraction (such as 95 percent) of repeated experiments.

Basic Type I and Type II errors?

Type I is a false positive (probability alpha); Type II is a false negative (beta); power is 1 minus beta.

Medium What does the central limit theorem say?

Sample means are approximately normal for large n regardless of the data's distribution, with standard error sigma over sqrt(n).

Medium How do you size an A/B test?

Pick the baseline rate, minimum detectable effect, alpha and power, then n per group is about 2 (1.96 + 0.84)^2 p (1 - p) / MDE^2 for proportions.

Medium Why is peeking at an A/B test a problem?

Repeated looks inflate the false positive rate; fix the sample size in advance or use sequential or Bayesian methods.

Medium What are multiple comparisons and how do you correct them?

Many tests produce false positives by chance. Use Bonferroni or Holm to control family-wise error or Benjamini-Hochberg to control false discovery rate.

Medium When use the bootstrap?

To estimate uncertainty of any statistic (median, AUC, F1) without distributional assumptions, resampling independent units with replacement.

Advanced Explain Simpson's paradox.

A trend appearing in every subgroup reverses in the aggregate because group sizes differ; analyse by segment and understand the causal structure.

Advanced Frequentist versus Bayesian A/B testing?

Frequentist tests give p-values and intervals about long-run error rates; Bayesian tests give the probability that B beats A and expected loss from a prior and the data, which is easier to interpret.

Advanced How do you compare two classifiers on the same test set?

Use a paired test on disagreements (McNemar or exact sign test) or a paired bootstrap, not just the accuracy difference.

Advanced What is CUPED?

A variance reduction method that adjusts the metric using pre-experiment covariates, shrinking confidence intervals and the sample needed.

Advanced What is a confounder and how do you handle it?

A variable affecting both treatment and outcome. Randomise, or adjust using matching, regression, propensity scores, instrumental variables or causal graphs.

LLM applications (13)

Basic Why do RAG systems fail?

Mostly in retrieval: documents missing from the index, poor chunking or embeddings, no reranking, weak query rewriting, or the model ignoring the evidence.

Basic When use RAG versus fine-tuning?

RAG for private or changing knowledge and citations; fine-tuning for style, format or behaviour at scale. They combine well.

Basic What is structured output and how do you make it reliable?

Model output constrained to a schema; use constrained decoding or function calling, validate, and retry with the error.

Medium What is hybrid search and why use it?

Combining BM25 and dense retrieval (for example with reciprocal rank fusion) captures both exact terms and semantic similarity.

Medium What does a reranker do?

A cross-encoder scores query and document together for the top candidates, improving precision over bi-encoder similarity.

Medium How do you chunk documents?

Structure-aware or semantic splits with modest overlap, retaining metadata, with parent-child retrieval or contextual summaries where chunks lose meaning alone.

Medium How do you evaluate an LLM application?

Build a labelled set of realistic cases, grade with code, rubrics and calibrated LLM judges, check retrieval separately, run in CI and monitor online metrics.

Medium Name biases of LLM-as-judge.

Preference for longer answers, for the first position and for its own style; mitigate with pairwise swaps, rubrics and calibration against human labels.

Advanced Design an agent loop and its safeguards.

Plan, call tools, observe, repeat with a step and cost budget, timeouts, validated tool arguments, human approval for risky actions, idempotent tools and full tracing.

Advanced What is Graph RAG?

Building a knowledge graph of entities and relations from documents to retrieve connected context or community summaries, helping multi-hop and corpus-wide questions.

Advanced How do you cut LLM cost and latency?

Prompt caching of stable prefixes, model routing and distillation, shorter prompts and outputs, batching, semantic caching, streaming and parallel tool calls.

Advanced How do you version and release prompt changes?

Treat prompts as code: store in version control, test against the evaluation set, canary or A/B release, pin model versions and keep rollback ready.

Advanced Long context or retrieval?

Long context is simple but costly and can lose information in the middle; retrieval scales to large corpora and is cheaper, but needs good indexing. Combine them: retrieve then place a concise context.
