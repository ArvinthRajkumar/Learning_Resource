Approach (8)

Basic How do you start a system design interview?

Clarify the problem: functional requirements, non-functional requirements (scale, latency, availability, consistency) and what is out of scope. Then estimate, define the API and data model, draw a high-level design, deep dive, and wrap up.

Basic What is the difference between functional and non-functional requirements?

Functional requirements describe what the system does (a user can post a photo). Non-functional requirements describe how well (p99 latency under 300 ms, 99.95 percent availability, durability, security).

Basic Why do we talk about p99 latency instead of the average?

Averages hide slow outliers. Users and fan-out calls experience the tail, so p95 and p99 reflect real experience far better.

Medium How do you decide between consistency and availability for a feature?

Ask what a wrong or missing answer costs. Money and unique identifiers need strong consistency. Feeds, likes and catalogue views tolerate staleness. Choose per operation, not for the whole system.

Medium What if you do not know a technology the interviewer mentions?

Say so, then reason from first principles: what problem it solves, what guarantees it needs, and compare with something you know. Honesty plus structured reasoning scores better than bluffing.

Medium How do you decide what to deep dive into?

Pick the components where the numbers are hardest or the requirements most demanding (the write path at scale, the hot partition, the consistency boundary), and ask the interviewer which they prefer.

Advanced How do you evolve a design when the interviewer changes a requirement mid-way?

State what changes (for example read to write ratio or consistency), identify the components affected, adjust only those with justified alternatives and say what you gave up. Show the design is modular.

Advanced What are signs of over-engineering in a design?

Technologies without a requirement or number behind them, sharding for a few hundred requests per second, many microservices for a small team, custom components where a managed one fits. Start from the simplest design that meets the numbers.

Estimation (9)

Basic Estimate the QPS of a service with 10 million daily active users each making 20 requests a day.

10 M x 20 = 200 M requests per day, divided by 86,400 seconds gives about 2,300 average QPS. With a peak factor of 3 that is about 7,000 QPS.

Basic How many seconds are in a day, and what do you use in estimates?

86,400. Round to 100,000 (10^5) to simplify, accepting about 14 percent error.

Basic What is 2^10, 2^20, 2^30, 2^40?

About a thousand (KB), a million (MB), a billion (GB), a trillion (TB).

Medium Compare memory, SSD and disk latencies.

Main memory about 100 ns, SSD random read about 100 microseconds (1,000 times slower), spinning disk seek about 10 ms (100,000 times slower than memory). Sequential access is far faster than random on all of them.

Medium How do you estimate storage for five years of data?

Items per day x size per item x 365 x 5 x replication factor, plus headroom for indexes and growth. Estimate media separately from text.

Medium What does 99.99 percent availability allow?

About 52 minutes of downtime per year, 4.4 minutes per month.

Medium How do you estimate cache size?

Take the hot set, often 20 percent of daily unique items, multiplied by item size, then add overhead. Check the expected hit rate against the load the database can take.

Advanced Two services in series each have 99.9 percent availability. What is the total, and how do you improve it?

About 99.8 percent. Improve with redundancy (parallel replicas across failure domains), fewer synchronous dependencies, caching and graceful degradation, remembering failures must be independent.

Advanced How do you sanity-check an estimate?

Compare with a known system, with single-machine capacity (does one box handle it?), and with unit costs. If the result implies absurd hardware, revisit assumptions.

Networking (9)

Basic What happens when you type a URL into a browser?

Cache check, DNS resolution, TCP handshake, TLS handshake, HTTP request, server processing (load balancer, application, cache, database), response, rendering and further requests.

Basic TCP versus UDP?

TCP is connection-oriented, reliable and ordered with congestion control. UDP is connectionless and best effort with lower overhead, used for DNS, real-time media and QUIC.

Basic What does DNS do and what is TTL?

It maps names to addresses through a hierarchy of resolvers and authoritative servers. TTL is how long an answer may be cached.

Medium Why is DNS-based failover slow?

Resolvers, operating systems and browsers cache answers beyond TTL and existing connections persist, so clients keep using a dead address for minutes.

Medium HTTP/1.1 versus HTTP/2 versus HTTP/3?

HTTP/1.1 uses one request at a time per connection. HTTP/2 multiplexes streams on one TCP connection. HTTP/3 runs over QUIC on UDP, removing TCP head-of-line blocking and speeding handshakes.

Medium Explain what a CDN does and how you invalidate content.

It caches content at edge locations near users, cutting latency and origin load. Invalidate by versioned file names (content hashes) or purge APIs, preferring versioning.

Medium WebSocket, SSE or long polling for real-time updates?

WebSocket for two-way low-latency (chat, collaboration), SSE for one-way server push over HTTP (feeds, notifications), long polling as a simple fallback.

Advanced Why does TLS add latency and how do you reduce it?

The handshake needs one to two round trips. Reduce with TLS 1.3, session resumption, connection reuse (keep-alive, HTTP/2), terminating TLS near users at a CDN or edge, and OCSP stapling.

Advanced What is anycast and where is it used?

Many locations announce the same IP and routing delivers a client to the nearest. DNS providers and CDNs use it for low latency and DDoS absorption.

Load balancing (9)

Basic What does a load balancer do?

Distributes requests across servers, performs health checks, removes failed servers, can terminate TLS and presents one stable address.

Basic Vertical versus horizontal scaling?

Vertical adds power to one machine (simple, limited, single point of failure). Horizontal adds machines (scales further, needs load balancing and stateless design).

Basic Why make web servers stateless?

Any instance can serve any request, so instances can be added, removed or replaced freely and failures lose no user state. State lives in shared stores or tokens.

Medium Layer 4 versus layer 7 load balancing?

L4 balances TCP or UDP connections by address and port, fast and simple. L7 understands HTTP and routes by path, header or cookie, terminates TLS and can apply auth and rate limits.

Medium Round robin versus least connections?

Round robin is simple and fine for uniform requests. Least connections adapts to uneven or long-lived requests by sending to the least busy server.

Medium What are sticky sessions and why avoid them?

The balancer pins a user to one server. It causes uneven load and loses session state on failure. Prefer a shared session store or client-side token.

Medium How do health checks go wrong?

Deep checks that depend on the database can eject the whole fleet during a blip. Use shallow checks, require several failures, and keep a minimum number of servers in rotation.

Advanced How do you make the load balancer itself highly available?

Run several in active-active across availability zones behind DNS or anycast, or an active-passive pair with a floating IP. Managed cloud load balancers do this for you.

Advanced What is the power of two choices?

Pick two random servers and send the request to the less loaded one. It nearly matches full least-load balancing with no global state and avoids herding.

Databases (10)

Basic SQL versus NoSQL: how do you choose?

From access patterns and consistency needs. Relational for joins, transactions and flexible queries. Key-value for fast lookups, document for nested entities, wide-column for massive writes, graph for relationships, search engines for text.

Basic What does ACID mean?

Atomicity (all or nothing), consistency (valid state to valid state), isolation (concurrent transactions do not interfere), durability (committed data survives crashes).

Basic What is an index and what does it cost?

A sorted structure (usually B-tree) that lets the database find rows without scanning the table. It speeds reads but uses space and slows writes.

Medium B-tree versus LSM tree?

B-trees update in place, with predictable reads and more random write I/O. LSM trees buffer writes in memory, flush sorted files and compact later, giving very fast writes and heavier read paths.

Medium What is the leftmost prefix rule for composite indexes?

An index on (a, b, c) supports queries filtering on a, on a and b, or on a, b and c, but not on b or c alone.

Medium When would you denormalise?

When read performance and simplicity for known queries outweigh the cost of duplicate data and more complex updates, typically at large scale or in NoSQL designs.

Medium OLTP versus OLAP?

OLTP handles many small transactions on row stores with millisecond latency. OLAP runs large scans and aggregations on column stores in warehouses. Keep analytics off the transactional database.

Advanced How does a write-ahead log provide durability?

The change is appended and flushed to a sequential log before data pages are modified. After a crash the database replays the log to redo committed work and undo uncommitted work. The log also feeds replication.

Advanced Why would you pick Cassandra and how do you model data in it?

For huge write throughput, linear scale and high availability. Model by query: one table per query with a partition key for the lookup and clustering columns for ordering, accepting duplication and no joins.

Advanced What is polyglot persistence and its risk?

Using different stores for different workloads. The risk is keeping them consistent, so declare one source of truth and propagate changes through events or change data capture.

Replication and sharding (11)

Basic Replication versus sharding?

Replication copies the same data to several nodes for availability, durability and read scaling. Sharding splits data across nodes for capacity and write scaling.

Basic What is leader-follower replication?

One leader accepts writes and ships a log to followers, which serve reads and take over if the leader fails.

Medium Synchronous versus asynchronous replication?

Synchronous waits for followers (no acknowledged data loss, higher latency, availability risk). Asynchronous is faster but can lose recent writes on leader failure. Semi-synchronous waits for one follower.

Medium What is replication lag and how do you cope?

Followers apply changes after the leader, so reads can be stale. Read your own writes from the leader, pin users to a replica for monotonic reads, and keep related writes in one partition.

Medium Range versus hash partitioning?

Range keeps ordered data and supports range scans but can create hot spots. Hash spreads load evenly but loses ordering and makes range queries scatter.

Medium What is consistent hashing and why use it?

Servers and keys are hashed onto a ring and a key belongs to the next server clockwise. Adding or removing a server moves only about 1/N of keys instead of nearly all.

Medium What are virtual nodes?

Many ring positions per physical server, which evens out load and spreads a failed server's load across many others.

Advanced Explain quorum reads and writes.

With N replicas, writes wait for W acknowledgements and reads query R replicas. If R + W > N the sets overlap, so a read sees the latest acknowledged write.

Advanced What is split brain and how do you prevent it?

Two nodes both act as leader after a partition or a false failure detection, accepting conflicting writes. Prevent with consensus-based leader election, leases and fencing tokens.

Advanced How do you reshard without downtime?

Add the new shard, dual write or ship the log, backfill, verify, switch reads then writes, then clean up, throttling the copy. Fixed many partitions or consistent hashing makes it cheaper.

Advanced What problems does sharding introduce?

Cross-shard joins and transactions, rebalancing, hot keys, global unique ids and constraints, and more operational complexity. Delay it until optimisation, replicas and caching are not enough.

Consistency (11)

Basic What is eventual consistency?

If no new writes happen, all replicas eventually converge to the same value. Reads in the meantime may be stale.

Basic State the CAP theorem.

During a network partition a distributed system must choose between consistency (linearizability) and availability. Normal operation has no such forced choice.

Medium What is PACELC?

If there is a Partition choose Availability or Consistency; Else choose Latency or Consistency. It captures the everyday trade-off CAP ignores.

Medium Name the isolation levels and anomalies.

Read uncommitted (dirty reads), read committed (no dirty reads), repeatable read (no non-repeatable reads, phantoms possible), serializable (no anomalies). Write skew and lost updates need care at lower levels.

Medium What is MVCC?

Multi-version concurrency control keeps several versions of rows so readers see a consistent snapshot without blocking writers.

Medium Two-phase commit versus saga?

2PC gives atomic commit across participants through prepare and commit with a coordinator, but blocks and holds locks. A saga runs local transactions with compensating actions, giving eventual consistency without distributed locks.

Advanced Linearizability versus serializability?

Linearizability is a recency guarantee for single objects (operations appear instant in real-time order). Serializability is an isolation guarantee for transactions (equivalent to some serial order). Strict serializability combines both.

Advanced Why are wall clocks unsafe for ordering events?

Clocks drift and are corrected, so timestamps from different machines may not reflect causality. Use logical or vector clocks, or bounded uncertainty such as Spanner's TrueTime.

Advanced Explain Raft briefly.

Nodes elect a leader per term by majority vote with randomised timeouts. The leader appends client commands to its log and replicates them; an entry commits when stored on a majority. A new leader always has all committed entries.

Advanced What is a fencing token?

A monotonically increasing number issued with each lock or lease. Storage rejects writes with a lower token, preventing a paused former lock holder from corrupting data.

Advanced What is a CRDT?

A data type whose replicas can be updated independently and merge deterministically without coordination, such as counters, sets and sequences. They give strong eventual consistency.

Caching (10)

Basic Why use a cache?

It serves repeated reads from fast memory, cutting latency and protecting the backend. It works because access patterns are skewed toward a hot set.

Basic Explain cache-aside.

The application checks the cache, on a miss reads the database and populates the cache with a TTL, and on writes updates the database then invalidates the key.

Medium Write-through versus write-back?

Write-through updates cache and database together (fresh, slower writes). Write-back writes to cache and persists later (fast, risks data loss if the cache fails before flushing).

Medium LRU versus LFU?

LRU evicts the least recently used item; LFU evicts the least frequently used. LRU adapts quickly; LFU preserves stable favourites but adapts slowly. TinyLFU combines both.

Medium What is a cache stampede and how do you prevent it?

A popular key expires and many requests rebuild it simultaneously. Prevent with request coalescing or locks, stale-while-revalidate, jittered TTLs and early refresh.

Medium Cache penetration and avalanche?

Penetration: repeated requests for non-existent keys bypass the cache; fix with negative caching or a Bloom filter. Avalanche: many keys expire together or the cache fails; fix with jitter, replication and rate limiting.

Medium Why delete rather than update the cache on write?

Updating can race with other writers and leave the cache holding an older value. Deleting lets the next read load the latest data.

Advanced How do you handle a hot key?

Replicate it across nodes with key suffixes, add a short in-process cache in front, and cache aggressively. Sharding alone does not help because one key lives on one node.

Advanced Redis versus Memcached?

Memcached is a simple multi-threaded string cache. Redis offers rich data types, persistence, replication, clustering, scripts and atomic operations, so it doubles as a lightweight data store.

Advanced How do you keep cache and database consistent?

Accept bounded staleness with TTLs, invalidate on writes, use change data capture to invalidate or update, and avoid dual writes. For strict needs read from the database or use versioned keys.

Messaging (11)

Basic Why use a message queue?

It decouples producers and consumers, absorbs spikes, enables retries, and lets slow work happen after returning to the user.

Basic Queue versus pub/sub?

A queue delivers each message to one consumer in a pool. Pub/sub delivers each message to every subscriber.

Medium At-most-once, at-least-once and exactly-once?

At most once may lose messages. At least once may duplicate. Exactly once in effect needs at-least-once plus idempotent consumers or transactional processing within one system.

Medium What is idempotency and how do you implement it?

Repeating an operation has the same effect as doing it once. Use idempotency keys with stored results, deduplication tables with unique constraints, natural idempotent updates, or conditional writes.

Medium What is a dead-letter queue?

A queue for messages that repeatedly fail, so poison messages do not block processing. Alert on its depth and provide a way to inspect and replay.

Medium How does Kafka differ from a traditional queue?

Kafka is a partitioned, replicated, retained log. Consumers track offsets, can replay, and many consumer groups read independently. Ordering holds within a partition.

Medium How do you preserve ordering?

Partition by the entity key so related events share a partition and process each partition sequentially. Global ordering is expensive and rarely needed.

Advanced Explain the transactional outbox.

Write the event to an outbox table in the same transaction as the business data. A relay or CDC publishes it afterwards, which avoids the dual-write inconsistency. Delivery is at least once so consumers deduplicate.

Advanced How do you handle backpressure?

Bound queues, autoscale consumers on lag or message age, rate limit or slow producers, and shed or sample low-value work. Monitor oldest message age.

Advanced Batch versus stream processing and watermarks?

Batch processes bounded data periodically; stream processes unbounded data continuously. Watermarks track event-time progress so windows can close despite late events.

Advanced Lambda versus Kappa architecture?

Lambda runs batch and speed layers and merges them (two code paths). Kappa uses only a stream layer and reprocesses by replaying the log.

APIs (11)

Basic What makes a good REST API?

Resource-oriented URLs, correct methods and status codes, consistent JSON and error shapes, pagination, versioning, idempotency for unsafe operations and clear documentation.

Basic Authentication versus authorization?

Authentication verifies who you are; authorization decides what you may do. Both must be checked on every request.

Medium REST versus gRPC versus GraphQL?

REST is simple and cacheable. gRPC is fast, typed and streaming for internal calls. GraphQL lets clients select fields to avoid over and under-fetching but complicates caching and query cost control.

Medium Offset versus cursor pagination?

Offset is simple but slow for large offsets and unstable when data changes. Cursor (keyset) pagination uses an index, is fast and stable but cannot jump to arbitrary pages.

Medium How do you design rate limiting?

Token bucket per client key at the gateway, with counters in Redis updated atomically, returning 429 with Retry-After, with different limits per endpoint and tier and a fail-open or fail-closed choice.

Medium What is an API gateway?

A single entry point handling TLS, authentication, rate limiting, routing and aggregation for many services, keeping business logic out.

Medium How does OAuth 2.0 authorization code flow work?

The user authenticates at the identity provider and consents; the app receives a short-lived code and exchanges it for access and refresh tokens, then calls the API with the bearer token.

Advanced Monolith versus microservices?

Start with a modular monolith. Move to microservices along business boundaries when team size or scaling demands it, accepting network failure modes, distributed data and operational cost.

Advanced How do services find each other?

Through service discovery: a registry (Consul, etcd) used by clients, or platform DNS and load balancers such as Kubernetes Services, with health checks and automatic deregistration.

Advanced What is a service mesh?

Sidecar proxies next to each service handling mutual TLS, retries, timeouts, circuit breaking, traffic splitting and telemetry without code changes, at the cost of complexity and latency.

Advanced How do you evolve an API without breaking clients?

Make additive changes, tolerate unknown fields, version breaking changes and support old versions for a deprecation window, and use contract tests.

Reliability (11)

Basic What are SLI, SLO and SLA?

SLI is a measured indicator, SLO is the internal target for it, SLA is the contractual commitment with penalties.

Basic What is a single point of failure?

A component whose failure takes down the system. Remove by redundancy across independent failure domains, with automatic failover.

Medium What is an error budget?

The allowed unreliability implied by the SLO (0.1 percent for 99.9). Spend it on velocity; when exhausted, prioritise reliability work.

Medium Explain timeouts, retries and backoff.

Every call needs a timeout. Retry only transient errors on idempotent operations with exponential backoff, jitter and a retry budget, at one layer only to avoid retry storms.

Medium How does a circuit breaker work?

After enough failures it opens and fails fast, then after a cool-down goes half-open to probe, closing again if the probe succeeds.

Medium What is a bulkhead?

Isolating resources (thread pools, queues, clusters) per dependency or tenant so one failure cannot exhaust everything.

Medium What is graceful degradation?

Keeping core functionality when parts fail by serving cached or default data, disabling optional features or switching to read-only mode.

Advanced How do cascading failures happen and how do you stop them?

A slow dependency fills caller pools and retries add load, spreading the failure. Stop with timeouts, circuit breakers, bulkheads, load shedding, retry budgets and capacity headroom.

Advanced RPO and RTO and DR strategies?

RPO is tolerable data loss; RTO is tolerable downtime. Strategies range from backup and restore (hours) through pilot light and warm standby to active-active multi-region (near zero) at rising cost.

Advanced How would you design for multi-region?

Route with GeoDNS or anycast, replicate data (asynchronous for latency, consensus for strictness), decide data ownership per region to avoid write conflicts, and test failover regularly.

Advanced How do you deploy safely?

Canary and blue/green releases, feature flags, automatic rollback on SLO breach, backward-compatible migrations, and small frequent changes.

Operations (10)

Basic What are logs, metrics and traces?

Logs are event records, metrics are aggregated numbers over time, traces follow one request across services. Together they provide observability.

Basic What are the golden signals?

Latency, traffic, errors and saturation.

Medium RED versus USE methods?

RED (rate, errors, duration) for request-driven services. USE (utilisation, saturation, errors) for resources like CPUs and disks.

Medium What makes a good alert?

It reflects user-visible symptoms, is actionable, urgent and linked to a runbook. Burn-rate alerts on SLOs are better than raw resource thresholds.

Medium How do you store passwords?

Salted slow hashes such as bcrypt, scrypt or Argon2, never plain text or fast hashes alone.

Medium How do you prevent SQL injection and XSS?

Parameterised queries and least privilege for SQL injection. Output encoding, content security policy and HttpOnly cookies for XSS.

Medium What is defence in depth and least privilege?

Layered controls so one failure is not fatal, and giving each identity only the access it needs.

Advanced Describe expand and contract migrations.

Add the new schema element, write both old and new, backfill, switch reads, then remove the old in a later release, so code and schema never need to change at the same instant.

Advanced Containers versus VMs and what Kubernetes provides?

Containers share the host kernel and are light and fast; VMs have stronger isolation. Kubernetes schedules containers, restarts failures, scales replicas, rolls out updates and provides service discovery.

Advanced How do you control cloud cost in a design?

Right-size, autoscale, use commitments or spot capacity, reduce egress with CDNs and locality, tier cold storage, and cache and compress.

Data systems (10)

Basic What is an inverted index?

A map from each term to the list of documents containing it, enabling fast full-text search.

Basic What is a Bloom filter and where is it used?

A compact probabilistic set with no false negatives and tunable false positives. Used to skip disk lookups for missing keys, check URLs and prevent cache penetration.

Medium How would you generate unique ids across shards?

UUIDs, time-ordered ids like Snowflake (timestamp, machine id, sequence), or a ticket server. Snowflake is compact, sortable and coordination free but depends on clocks and unique machine ids.

Medium How do you count unique visitors at scale?

HyperLogLog gives about 1 percent error in kilobytes and sketches merge across shards; exact counts need large sets or sampling.

Medium How does geospatial search work?

Cells such as geohash, quadtree, S2 or H3 index locations. Query the target cell and its neighbours, then filter by exact distance.

Medium Data warehouse versus data lake?

A warehouse holds modelled, structured data with schema on write. A lake stores raw files cheaply with schema on read. Lakehouses add table formats and transactions to lakes.

Advanced How do you scale search?

Shard the index by document, replicate shards, query all shards and merge top results, and keep the index as a derived view updated via queue or CDC.

Advanced What is change data capture?

Reading a database's transaction log and publishing each change as an event, feeding caches, search indexes and warehouses without dual writes.

Advanced How do you build a distributed lock safely?

Use a lease with expiry and fencing tokens on a consensus-backed store, or avoid locks with idempotency, ownership by partition or optimistic versioning.

Advanced Why use erasure coding for object storage?

It stores data plus parity pieces and survives multiple losses at lower overhead than keeping three full copies.

Case studies 1 (8)

Basic Design a URL shortener: how do you generate the key?

Use base62 on a counter or pre-generated unique ids, or hash and handle collisions. Seven base62 characters give 3.5 trillion keys.

Medium 301 or 302 for the redirect?

302 keeps traffic flowing through you for analytics and changeable targets. 301 is cached by browsers and cuts load but loses control.

Medium Design Pastebin: where does the content go?

In object storage behind a CDN, with metadata in a database and expiry handled by stored timestamps and lifecycle rules.

Medium Design a rate limiter: which algorithm and where is state?

Token bucket at the gateway with atomic counters in Redis per client key, with rules in a cached config service.

Medium Design a distributed cache.

Sharded nodes with consistent hashing, LRU and TTL eviction per node, replicas for failover, client library with timeouts and circuit breaker, hot-key handling and request coalescing.

Advanced How do you avoid races in a distributed rate limiter?

Make the check and update atomic using Redis INCR or a Lua script, or accept approximate limits with local buckets that sync periodically.

Advanced Design a key-value store with high availability.

Consistent hashing with virtual nodes, replication to N nodes, tunable quorums, vector clocks, hinted handoff, Merkle-tree anti-entropy, gossip membership and LSM storage.

Advanced How does hinted handoff work?

When a replica is down, another node accepts the write with a hint and delivers it when the replica returns, keeping writes available.

Case studies 2 (8)

Basic News feed: fan-out on read or write?

Write fan-out gives fast reads for most users; read fan-out avoids huge write amplification for celebrities. Use a hybrid.

Medium How do you store and paginate a feed?

Store post ids in per-user sorted sets (capped), hydrate content from caches in batch, and paginate with a cursor on id or time.

Medium Design chat: how are messages delivered and ordered?

Persistent WebSocket gateways with a session registry, messages persisted and sequenced per conversation, push for offline users, clients sync by last sequence.

Medium Design a notification system.

Intake API with idempotency, preference and template services, per-channel queues and worker pools with provider rate limits, retries, DLQs, priorities and delivery tracking.

Medium Design typeahead.

A trie with precomputed top-k per node served from memory and caches, rebuilt periodically from aggregated query logs, with client debouncing and a trending overlay.

Advanced How do you implement presence at scale?

Client heartbeats set a TTL key in Redis; absence means offline. Fetch or subscribe only for visible contacts to avoid broadcast storms.

Advanced Design a web crawler.

A prioritised URL frontier with per-host queues for politeness, distributed fetchers, DNS and robots caches, URL dedupe with a Bloom filter, content hashing, trap limits and recrawl scheduling.

Advanced How do you prevent a crawler from overloading a site?

One queue per host, a minimum delay between requests, obey robots.txt and Crawl-delay, and assign hosts to workers by hash so politeness is local.

Case studies 3 (8)

Medium Design video streaming: how does playback adapt to bandwidth?

Videos are transcoded into segments at several bitrates with a manifest; the player downloads segments over HTTP via a CDN and switches rendition per segment based on throughput and buffer.

Medium How do you speed up video processing?

Split into segments, transcode in parallel with a workflow engine, retry failed segments independently and write outputs to object storage.

Medium Design Dropbox: how do you sync large files efficiently?

Block-level chunking with hashes gives delta sync and deduplication; metadata in a relational store; clients notified of changes and conflicts resolved with versions.

Medium What is a double-entry ledger?

Each movement of money is two entries, a debit and a credit, summing to zero. Entries are append-only and balances are derived, giving auditability.

Advanced Design Uber: how do you match riders and drivers?

Maintain driver locations in a geospatial index in memory, query nearby cells, rank by ETA, reserve the driver atomically, and track the trip with a state machine.

Advanced Design ticket booking: how do you avoid double booking?

Atomic conditional updates or row locks on seats, a hold with expiry, a payment step and a waiting room to absorb spikes.

Advanced Design a payment system.

Idempotency keys, a payment state machine, tokenised cards via a PSP, a double-entry append-only ledger, webhook handling and reconciliation, with strong consistency.

Advanced A payment provider call times out. What now?

Treat the outcome as unknown: query the provider using the idempotency key or reference, then proceed. Never blindly retry with a new request.

Case studies 4 (7)

Medium Design a metrics system: pull or push?

Pull gives liveness knowledge and central config; push suits short-lived jobs. Buffer through a queue, store in a compressed time-series database with downsampling and cardinality limits.

Medium Design a leaderboard.

A Redis sorted set with ZADD, ZREVRANK and ZREVRANGE for updates, rank and top-k; separate sets per time window; database as the source of truth.

Advanced Design ad click aggregation with accurate counts.

Kafka partitioned by ad id, stream windows with event time and watermarks, checkpoints and idempotent writes, raw events archived and a batch job reconciling numbers for billing.

Advanced How do you handle a hot key in aggregation?

Add a salt to the key, aggregate partial counts, then merge in a second stage.

Advanced How would you design Kafka?

Partitioned append-only logs with leaders and followers, in-sync replicas and acks, consumer groups with committed offsets, retention and compaction, and a Raft-based controller.

Advanced Design Google Docs collaboration.

WebSocket sessions with one server per document, operational transformation or CRDTs for convergence, optimistic local application, operation log with snapshots and ephemeral presence.

Advanced OT versus CRDT?

OT transforms concurrent operations with a central ordering; CRDTs give every element a unique id so operations commute and no server is needed, at the cost of metadata.

Low-level design (8)

Basic What are the SOLID principles?

Single responsibility, open/closed, Liskov substitution, interface segregation and dependency inversion.

Basic Composition versus inheritance?

Prefer composition: it is more flexible and avoids tight coupling and deep hierarchies. Use inheritance for real is-a relationships.

Medium When do you use the Strategy pattern?

When an algorithm should be swappable at runtime, such as pricing, routing or eviction policy, replacing conditionals with polymorphism.

Medium State pattern example?

A vending machine or order lifecycle where behaviour depends on state; each state encapsulates its behaviour and transitions.

Medium Design a parking lot.

Entities: ParkingLot, Floor, Spot, Vehicle, Ticket, Pricing strategy. Allocate best-fit spots, issue a ticket, compute fee on exit; extend with new vehicle types and pricing rules without modifying core code.

Medium Why is Singleton often discouraged?

It is global mutable state that hides dependencies and complicates testing. Inject a single shared instance instead.

Advanced How do you make a class thread safe?

Protect shared mutable state with locks or atomics, prefer immutability and message passing, keep critical sections small, avoid deadlocks with consistent lock ordering.

Advanced How do you make an LRU cache scale across threads?

Shard keys into independent LRU segments each with its own lock (striped locking) to reduce contention.

Storage engines (15)

Basic Why do databases store data in pages?

Disks and SSDs read and write in blocks. Fixed-size pages (4 to 16 KB) match that unit, so each I/O fetches many records and the buffer pool can cache them.

Basic What is a write-ahead log and why does every durable database have one?

An append-only record of changes written and flushed before the data pages are modified. After a crash the database replays it, so committed changes are not lost and half-written changes can be undone.

Basic How deep is a B-tree for a billion keys?

With a fan-out of several hundred, three to four levels; the upper levels stay cached, so a lookup costs about one or two disk reads.

Medium B-tree versus LSM tree: when do you choose each?

B-trees for read-heavy and transactional workloads with range queries and predictable latency; LSM trees for write-heavy or ingestion workloads, accepting read and space amplification and background compaction.

Medium How does an LSM tree handle a write and a read?

A write goes to the WAL and the in-memory memtable; full memtables flush to immutable sorted SSTables. A read checks the memtable then SSTables from newest to oldest, using bloom filters to skip files.

Medium What is a tombstone and why are deletes slow to reclaim space in LSM engines?

A delete writes a marker because SSTables are immutable. The old value and the marker are physically removed only when compaction merges the files that contain them.

Medium What is write amplification?

The ratio of bytes physically written to storage to bytes the application wrote. LSM compaction and B-tree page rewrites both cause it; it affects SSD lifetime and throughput.

Medium How does a bloom filter reduce reads in an LSM tree?

Each SSTable has a filter that can say a key is definitely absent, so most lookups for missing keys skip that file with no disk access.

Medium Why can random UUID primary keys hurt a B-tree?

Inserts land on random leaf pages, causing cache misses and page splits and fragmentation. Time-ordered ids append at the right edge and keep locality.

Advanced Explain compaction strategies.

Size-tiered merges similar-sized files (cheap writes, more space and read amplification); leveled keeps non-overlapping files per level (better reads and space, higher write amplification); time-window compaction suits TTL time series.

Advanced What is the RUM conjecture?

A storage design cannot minimise read cost, update cost and memory/space at the same time; optimising two worsens the third.

Advanced Why are column stores faster for analytics?

They read only the needed columns, compress similar values well (run-length, dictionary, delta) and use vectorised execution. They are slower for single-row transactional access.

Advanced What does group commit do?

Batches the log flushes of many concurrent transactions into one fsync, greatly improving throughput at a small latency cost.

Advanced How does MVCC let readers avoid blocking writers?

Each write creates a new row version tagged with transaction ids; a reader sees a snapshot of versions committed before it began. Old versions are cleaned later (vacuum or purge).

Advanced What is a checkpoint?

A point where dirty pages are flushed so recovery needs to replay the log only from that point forward, bounding recovery time and allowing old log segments to be removed.

Event-driven design (15)

Basic Event versus command?

A command asks one handler to do something and can be rejected. An event records something that already happened and is broadcast to any interested consumer.

Basic Why do message consumers need to be idempotent?

Brokers deliver at least once, so retries, rebalances and relay crashes cause duplicates. Idempotent handlers make duplicates harmless.

Medium What is the dual-write problem and how does the outbox pattern solve it?

Updating a database and publishing to a broker are two systems, so a crash can lose one. The outbox writes the event into a table in the same local transaction, and a relay publishes it afterwards.

Medium How do you achieve effectively exactly-once processing?

At-least-once delivery plus deduplication: store processed message ids in the same transaction as the effect, or make the operation naturally idempotent.

Medium Explain CQRS and when it is worth it.

Separate write and read models, with projections updated from events. Worth it when read and write shapes or scale differ greatly; not for simple CRUD because of eventual consistency and extra moving parts.

Medium What is event sourcing and what are its costs?

Storing every state change as an immutable event and deriving state by replay. Benefits: audit and time travel. Costs: projections for queries, schema evolution of old events, and handling deletion requests.

Medium Saga: orchestration versus choreography?

Orchestration uses a central coordinator that calls steps and triggers compensations; choreography has services react to each other's events. Orchestration is easier to see and debug; choreography is more decoupled.

Medium How do you preserve ordering in a partitioned log?

Use the entity id as the partition key, so that all events for one entity go to one partition. Ordering is not guaranteed across partitions.

Medium What is a dead letter queue?

A place to put messages that exceed their retry limit, so a poison message does not block the stream and can be inspected and replayed.

Advanced How do you evolve event schemas safely?

Use a schema registry with compatibility rules: add optional fields, never reuse or retype fields, version event types, and upcast old events on read.

Advanced What are snapshots in event sourcing?

Periodic captures of aggregate state so rebuilding replays only the events after the latest snapshot.

Advanced How do you prevent two writers corrupting an event-sourced aggregate?

Optimistic concurrency: appends carry the expected version and are rejected if the stream has moved on.

Advanced What is consumer lag and why monitor it?

The gap between the latest offset and what a consumer group has processed. Growing lag means consumers cannot keep up, so scale them, batch, or investigate slow handlers.

Advanced Why is exactly-once delivery impossible over a network, and what does Kafka claim?

A sender cannot distinguish a lost message from a lost acknowledgement. Kafka offers idempotent producers and transactions within Kafka; end-to-end effects still need idempotent consumers.

Advanced When should you avoid an event-driven design?

When you need an immediate response and strong consistency, in small systems, or when the team cannot yet operate brokers, tracing and replays.

Data processing (14)

Basic Batch versus stream processing?

Batch processes bounded datasets with high latency and simple reasoning; stream processes unbounded events continuously with low latency and needs state and time handling.

Basic Explain MapReduce.

Map transforms records into key-value pairs, the shuffle groups pairs by key across the network, and reduce aggregates each key's values. Failed tasks are simply rerun.

Medium What is data skew and how do you fix it?

A few keys carry most of the data so one task straggles. Fix by salting hot keys, broadcasting the small side of a join, filtering nulls or splitting heavy keys.

Medium Why is Spark often faster than MapReduce?

It keeps intermediate data in memory, pipelines narrow transformations, and optimises whole DAGs, instead of writing to disk between every stage.

Medium Narrow versus wide transformations?

Narrow ones (map, filter) work within a partition and are pipelined. Wide ones (join, groupBy) need a shuffle and start a new stage.

Medium What are event time, processing time and watermarks?

Event time is when it happened, processing time when it is seen. A watermark estimates how far event time has progressed so windows can close, trading completeness against latency.

Medium Lambda versus Kappa architecture?

Lambda runs batch and speed layers and merges them, duplicating logic. Kappa uses only a stream processor over a replayable log and reprocesses by replay.

Medium Why use Parquet instead of CSV for analytics?

Columnar layout, compression, schema and statistics enable column pruning and predicate pushdown, reading far less data.

Advanced How does Flink provide exactly-once state?

Periodic distributed checkpoints (barrier snapshots) of operator state and source offsets; on failure it restores the last checkpoint and replays. Sinks need transactions or idempotence for exactly-once effects.

Advanced Name sketches for approximate analytics.

Bloom filters (membership), HyperLogLog (distinct counts), Count-Min sketch (frequencies), t-digest (quantiles); small, mergeable and with bounded error.

Advanced How would you count unique visitors across billions of events per day?

HyperLogLog per dimension, merged across partitions; about 12 KB per counter gives around 2% error. Use exact counts only where required.

Advanced What problem do Delta Lake, Iceberg and Hudi solve?

ACID transactions, concurrent writes, schema evolution, time travel and efficient upserts on files in object storage.

Advanced Why must pipeline tasks be idempotent and partitioned by date?

Retries and backfills rerun tasks; overwriting a deterministic partition gives the same result without duplicates.

Advanced How do you join a huge fact table with a small dimension?

Broadcast join: send the small table to all workers and join locally, avoiding a shuffle of the large table.

Cloud and deployment (14)

Basic Region versus availability zone?

A region is a geographic area; an availability zone is an isolated data centre inside it. Multi-AZ survives a data centre failure; multi-region survives a regional outage.

Basic Container versus virtual machine?

A container shares the host kernel and packages the app with its dependencies, so it is lighter and faster to start; a VM includes a full guest operating system.

Basic What is infrastructure as code?

Declaring infrastructure in version-controlled files (Terraform, CloudFormation) so it is reviewable, reproducible and drift is detectable.

Medium What does Kubernetes do?

Schedules containers across machines, keeps the declared number of replicas running, load-balances Services, rolls out updates and restarts failed pods by reconciling actual state to desired state.

Medium Liveness versus readiness probes?

Liveness failing causes a restart; readiness failing removes the pod from load balancing until it is ready.

Medium Blue-green versus canary deployments?

Blue-green switches all traffic between two full environments for instant rollback at double capacity; canary exposes a small share first and expands as metrics stay healthy.

Medium How do you deploy a breaking database change safely?

Expand and contract: add the new structure, write both, backfill, move reads, then remove the old. Every step stays compatible with the running code.

Medium How does target-tracking autoscaling decide replicas?

desired = ceil(current replicas x current metric / target metric), within min and max, with cooldowns to avoid flapping.

Medium Define RPO and RTO and relate them to DR patterns.

RPO is tolerable data loss, RTO tolerable downtime. Backup-restore is cheapest and slowest; pilot light, warm standby and active-active reduce both at rising cost.

Advanced What are the hidden costs in a cloud bill?

Cross-AZ and cross-region traffic, NAT gateway processing, egress, log and metric ingestion, idle resources and over-provisioned storage.

Advanced Serverless: strengths and limits?

No server management and pay-per-use scaling to zero; but cold starts, execution time limits, vendor lock-in, and unpredictable cost at sustained high load.

Advanced How would you design for a regional failure?

Replicate data across regions (async or sync depending on RPO), keep stateless services deployable in both, use health-checked global routing, and rehearse failover regularly.

Advanced Why immutable infrastructure and one artifact for all environments?

It removes configuration drift and makes what you tested what you ship; environment differences live in configuration and secrets.

Advanced What are spot instances good for?

Interruptible, fault-tolerant work such as batch jobs, CI and stateless workers, at large discounts, with checkpointing and graceful handling of reclaim notices.

Security architecture (15)

Basic Authentication versus authorization?

Authentication verifies who you are; authorization determines what you may do.

Basic How should passwords be stored?

As salted hashes from a slow, memory-hard function such as argon2id, scrypt or bcrypt. Never plaintext, never fast hashes like MD5 or plain SHA-256.

Basic How do you prevent SQL injection?

Parameterised queries or prepared statements so user input is bound as data, plus least-privileged database accounts.

Medium Sessions versus JWTs?

Sessions keep state on the server and are easy to revoke; JWTs are self-contained and verifiable by any service but hard to revoke, so use short lifetimes and refresh tokens.

Medium Explain the OAuth 2.0 authorization code flow with PKCE.

The app redirects to the authorization server with a code challenge; after login it receives a one-time code and exchanges it with the code verifier for tokens, so an intercepted code is useless.

Medium OAuth versus OpenID Connect?

OAuth delegates authorization with access tokens; OIDC adds an ID token and standard claims to authenticate the user.

Medium What is IDOR/BOLA and how do you prevent it?

Accessing another user's object by changing an id. Enforce object-level authorization on the server for every request.

Medium Symmetric versus asymmetric encryption?

Symmetric (AES) uses one shared key and is fast for bulk data; asymmetric (RSA, ECC) uses key pairs for key exchange and signatures. TLS combines both.

Medium What is envelope encryption?

Data is encrypted with a data key, which is encrypted by a master key in a KMS or HSM. Rotating the master key re-wraps small data keys instead of re-encrypting all data.

Advanced What is zero trust?

No implicit trust from network location: every request is authenticated, authorized and encrypted, with device and identity checks, microsegmentation and least privilege.

Advanced How do you secure service-to-service calls?

mTLS (often via a service mesh) for mutual authentication, short-lived workload identities, per-service authorization policies and network policies.

Advanced Explain CSRF and its defences.

A malicious site makes the user's browser send an authenticated request. Defend with SameSite cookies, anti-CSRF tokens and checking Origin headers.

Advanced How do you manage secrets?

Store in a secret manager, inject at runtime, scope narrowly, rotate, audit and prefer short-lived credentials such as workload identity over static keys; never commit them.

Advanced Walk through STRIDE on a login endpoint.

Spoofing (credential stuffing, MFA), Tampering (TLS, signed tokens), Repudiation (audit logs), Information disclosure (generic errors, no secrets in logs), Denial of service (rate limits), Elevation (authorization checks).

Advanced How do you defend an API against abuse?

Authentication, per-key rate limits and quotas, WAF and bot detection, input validation and size limits, idempotency keys, monitoring and anomaly alerts.

ML systems design (14)

Basic What are the stages of an ML system?

Define objective and metrics, collect and label data, build features, train and evaluate, deploy and serve, monitor and retrain.

Basic Batch versus online inference?

Batch precomputes predictions on a schedule and stores them; online computes per request with latency limits. Choose by freshness needs and cost.

Medium What is training-serving skew and how do you avoid it?

Features computed differently in training and production silently hurt accuracy. Use shared feature definitions, a feature store and logging served features for training.

Medium What does a feature store provide?

Consistent feature definitions, an offline store for training with point-in-time joins, an online store for low-latency serving, and a registry with ownership and freshness.

Medium Explain the recommendation funnel.

Candidate generation narrows millions of items to hundreds cheaply, ranking scores them with a richer model, and re-ranking applies diversity, business rules and freshness.

Medium How do you handle cold start?

Use content features, popularity and context, explore with bandits, and ask for explicit preferences; update quickly as interactions arrive.

Medium How do you decide a new model is better?

Offline metrics on a held-out, time-split set, then an online A/B test on business metrics with guardrail metrics and enough statistical power.

Medium What is data drift and how do you detect it?

The input distribution changes over time. Monitor feature statistics with PSI or KS tests, and track prediction distributions and delayed label performance.

Advanced How do you serve a model at 20,000 requests per second?

Estimate per-instance throughput, use dynamic batching, quantisation or distillation, autoscaling on queue depth, caching, a fallback model and canary deployments.

Advanced Explain approximate nearest neighbour search.

Exact search is O(N d). ANN indexes such as HNSW, IVF and PQ trade a little recall for large speedups and memory savings.

Advanced Design a RAG system.

Ingest, chunk and embed documents with permissions; at query time rewrite, retrieve (vector plus keyword), re-rank, build a prompt, generate with citations, and apply guardrails, caching and evaluation.

Advanced How do you control LLM application cost and latency?

Trim context, cache prompts and results, route simple requests to smaller models, stream output, set token limits and batch offline work.

Advanced What is a feedback loop problem in recommenders?

The model is trained on what it previously showed, reinforcing its own biases. Counter with exploration, randomised traffic and counterfactual evaluation.

Advanced How do you avoid label leakage?

Ensure features use only information available at prediction time, split data by time, and make joins point-in-time correct.

Case studies 5 (14)

Basic Why is a geohash useful for nearby search?

It turns two-dimensional location into a string whose shared prefix means nearness, so proximity becomes a prefix lookup in a standard index.

Medium Why search neighbouring cells as well as the target cell?

Two close points can fall on opposite sides of a cell boundary, so query the cell and its eight neighbours, then filter by exact distance.

Medium Quadtree versus geohash?

A quadtree splits cells by density so crowded areas get finer cells; geohash cells are uniform per precision. Quadtrees live in memory; geohash works in any key-value store.

Medium How are map tiles served?

Pre-rendered or vector tiles per zoom level and x/y coordinates, stored as static files and served from a CDN.

Medium How do you prevent overselling in checkout?

Atomic conditional updates (UPDATE ... WHERE available > 0 and check rows affected), row locks or compare-and-set, with reservations that expire.

Medium How do you make payment calls safe to retry?

Send an idempotency key per payment attempt; on timeout reconcile with the provider before retrying, since the true outcome is unknown.

Medium Why is hotel inventory modelled per night?

A stay spans nights; the booking must decrement availability for every night atomically, which a per-night inventory row allows with a transaction.

Medium What is overbooking and why do hotels use it?

Selling more than capacity, expecting cancellations and no-shows, with a policy for handling the rare overflow.

Advanced How do routing services avoid running Dijkstra on the whole road graph?

Preprocessing such as contraction hierarchies or hub labels, A* and bidirectional search, and regional partitioning, with live traffic as updated edge weights.

Advanced Design the checkout flow as a saga.

Reserve inventory, create order, charge payment, confirm; on failure, compensate by releasing stock and cancelling or refunding, each step idempotent and driven by events.

Advanced How do you handle a flash sale with 5 million users and 10,000 items?

CDN and waiting room, bot and rate limits, atomic Redis stock decrement so only winners continue, queued order processing, expiry of unpaid reservations, and reconciliation with the database.

Advanced How do you separate search from booking consistency in a hotel system?

Search uses a denormalised, slightly stale index; booking re-checks availability in the strongly consistent database under a transaction.

Advanced How would you shard a proximity service?

By region or geohash prefix so queries hit one or a few shards, balancing dense areas by splitting cells; replicate for availability.

Advanced How do you compute ETA?

Sum predicted traversal times of road segments using historical profiles and live speeds from map-matched location pings, refined by ML and updated while driving.

Case studies 6 (14)

Basic Why are UUIDs sometimes a poor primary key?

Random 128-bit values are large and scatter inserts across a B-tree. Time-ordered ids such as ULID, UUIDv7 or Snowflake give locality.

Medium Describe the Snowflake id layout.

1 sign bit, 41 bits of milliseconds since a custom epoch, 10 bits of worker id and 12 bits of sequence, giving 4096 ids per millisecond per worker.

Medium What goes wrong if the clock moves backwards in a Snowflake generator?

It could repeat timestamps and produce duplicate ids. Detect it, wait or refuse, and keep the last timestamp.

Medium Why does a lease-based distributed lock need fencing tokens?

A paused holder can wake after its lease expired and write while another holds the lock. The resource rejects tokens older than the newest seen.

Medium How do multiple scheduler instances avoid running the same job twice?

Partition jobs by hash and claim jobs with a conditional update that sets a lease, so only one instance wins; jobs are still idempotent for lease expiry.

Medium How are retries handled in a job scheduler?

Exponential backoff with jitter, an attempt counter and a dead letter state after the limit.

Medium How does a code judge keep untrusted code safe?

Containers or microVMs with cgroup CPU and memory limits, seccomp filters, no network, read-only filesystems and wall-clock timeouts, with fresh sandboxes per run.

Medium How does a client read a file in HDFS?

It asks the NameNode for block locations, then reads blocks directly from the nearest DataNodes; data does not flow through the NameNode.

Advanced Redis lock versus ZooKeeper or etcd lock?

Redis is fast but not consensus-based, so failover can lose a lock. ZooKeeper and etcd use consensus and provide stronger guarantees and revision numbers usable as fencing tokens.

Advanced Why is an exchange matching engine single-threaded per symbol?

Price-time priority needs strict deterministic ordering, and avoiding locks gives microsecond latency. Throughput scales by sharding symbols.

Advanced How does a matching engine recover from a crash?

Replay the sequenced input journal into a fresh book (event sourcing), or fail over to a hot standby that processed the same sequence.

Advanced How does HDFS stay available when nodes die?

Blocks are replicated (3 by default) across racks; missed heartbeats trigger re-replication; the NameNode has a standby with a shared edit log.

Advanced Why does HDFS struggle with many small files?

The NameNode keeps all metadata in memory, so millions of small files exhaust it; pack them into larger containers.

Advanced Replication versus erasure coding?

Replication (3x) is simple and fast to read; erasure coding (for example 6+3) cuts overhead to 1.5x for cold data but makes reconstruction costlier.

Trade-offs and mocks (13)

Basic How would you structure a 45-minute system design interview?

Requirements and scope (5 min), estimates (5), API and data model (7), high-level design (10), deep dives (12), failures, scaling and wrap-up (6).

Basic Roughly how much downtime does 99.9% availability allow per year?

About 8.8 hours; 99.99% allows about 52 minutes.

Medium What if you do not know a number the interviewer asks for?

State a reasonable assumption out loud, label it, and show how the design would change if it were different.

Medium How do you decide between SQL and NoSQL?

Start from access patterns, consistency needs and scale: relational for transactions and ad hoc queries; wide-column or key-value for massive partitioned writes with known queries.

Medium When would you choose a queue over a synchronous call?

When the work can be delayed, bursts need smoothing, there is fan-out, or you want to decouple failures from the caller.

Medium How do you show that you considered failure?

For each component say what happens if it is down, slow or wrong, and name the mitigation: replication, retries with idempotency, timeouts, circuit breakers, backups.

Medium How do you scale a design from 1x to 10x?

Find the first bottleneck from the numbers (CPU, database writes, network, memory), then apply the cheapest remedy: cache, replicas, partitioning, queues, or bigger machines.

Advanced How would you design chat delivery to be reliable?

Per-conversation sequence numbers, client acknowledgements, resend from the last seen sequence on reconnect, and idempotent message ids to deduplicate retries.

Advanced How do you handle billing for an API platform?

Emit idempotent usage events asynchronously, aggregate in a stream processor, store immutable records, and reconcile against gateway logs.

Advanced Give an example of trading consistency for availability.

A shopping cart or feed can accept writes on any replica and converge later, while payments and inventory choose strong consistency.

Advanced What are the signs of an over-engineered design?

Microservices, sharding and multi-region for modest numbers, many technologies without reasons, and no discussion of operational cost.

Advanced How do you incorporate cost into a design?

Estimate compute, storage and egress, identify the dominant cost, and pick levers such as caching, tiering, reserved capacity and compression.

Advanced How do you respond if the interviewer challenges a choice?

Restate their concern, compare the alternative on the requirement that matters, and either defend with numbers or adapt with a clear explanation of the change.

Glossary and terminology (14)

Basic Define latency and throughput.

Latency is the time for one request; throughput is the amount of work completed per unit time.

Basic SLI, SLO and SLA?

The measured indicator, the target set for it, and the contractual commitment with penalties.

Basic What is idempotency?

Repeating an operation has the same effect as doing it once, enabling safe retries.

Basic What is a CDN?

A network of edge caches near users that serves static and cacheable content with low latency.

Medium What is eventual consistency?

If no new updates are made, all replicas eventually converge to the same value.

Medium What is a quorum?

A majority or configured number of replicas that must respond; R + W > N makes reads and writes overlap.

Medium What is consistent hashing?

A hashing scheme on a ring where adding or removing a node moves only a small share of keys.

Medium What is a circuit breaker?

A guard that stops calling a failing dependency for a while, failing fast and letting it recover.

Medium What is backpressure?

A mechanism for slow consumers to tell producers to slow down instead of being overwhelmed.

Medium What is a cache stampede and how do you prevent it?

Many requests rebuild an expired hot entry at once. Use request coalescing or locks, jittered TTLs, and refresh-ahead.

Medium What is split brain?

Two nodes both act as leader after a partition, risking conflicting writes. Prevent with quorum-based election and fencing.

Advanced Differentiate CAP and PACELC.

CAP covers behaviour during partitions (consistency or availability); PACELC adds that without partitions there is still a trade-off between latency and consistency.

Advanced What is a CRDT?

A data type whose concurrent updates merge deterministically without coordination, such as counters, sets and registers.

Advanced What is a service mesh?

A layer of sidecar proxies that provide mTLS, retries, routing and telemetry for service-to-service calls without changing application code.

Consensus and time (14)

Basic Why can't distributed machines rely on wall-clock time to order events?

Clocks drift and can jump when corrected, so two machines disagree. Ordering by timestamps can drop newer writes; use logical or hybrid clocks, or version vectors.

Basic What is a quorum?

A majority (or configured number) of replicas that must participate. Any two majorities overlap, so conflicting decisions cannot both succeed.

Medium Explain Lamport clocks.

Each node keeps a counter, increments it per event, stamps messages, and on receive sets it to max(local, received) + 1. If A happened before B then its stamp is smaller; the reverse does not hold.

Medium What do vector clocks add?

One counter per node lets you decide whether two versions are ordered or concurrent, which detects true conflicts (as in Dynamo-style stores).

Medium Describe Raft in three parts.

Leader election with terms and randomised timeouts, log replication where an entry commits once a majority stores it, and safety rules ensuring only up-to-date candidates win and committed entries are never lost.

Medium Why use odd cluster sizes for consensus?

Majorities of 2n+1 nodes tolerate n failures; adding one more node (2n+2) needs a larger majority and tolerates no more failures.

Medium What happens in Raft when the leader is partitioned from the majority?

It cannot commit new entries. The majority side elects a new leader with a higher term; when the partition heals the old leader steps down and its uncommitted entries are discarded.

Medium Why must stale leaders not serve reads blindly?

A deposed leader may not know it was replaced and return old data. Reads go through the log, use a leader lease with bounded clock drift, or confirm leadership with a quorum.

Advanced State the FLP result and how real systems cope.

In a fully asynchronous system consensus cannot be guaranteed if even one node may crash. Real protocols assume partial synchrony and use timeouts and randomisation, giving up liveness (not safety) during bad periods.

Advanced Paxos versus Raft?

Both give consensus with majorities. Paxos separates proposers, acceptors and learners with two phases and is notoriously subtle; Raft decomposes the problem around a strong leader and a replicated log and is easier to implement and teach.

Advanced What is a CRDT and where is it used?

A data type whose merge is commutative, associative and idempotent so replicas converge without coordination: counters, sets, registers, collaborative text. Used in active-active databases and editors, with limits on enforceable invariants.

Advanced Hybrid logical clocks: what and why?

They combine physical time with a logical counter so timestamps stay near real time but still respect causality; used by distributed databases to order transactions without special hardware.

Advanced How does gossip detect failures and spread membership?

Nodes periodically exchange state with random peers so information reaches all nodes in O(log N) rounds; failure detectors such as phi accrual or SWIM turn missed heartbeats into suspicion levels.

Advanced Why does Spanner use atomic clocks?

TrueTime exposes a bounded uncertainty interval; Spanner waits out the uncertainty before committing so timestamps give external consistency across the globe.

Web delivery (14)

Basic What happens when you type a URL and press enter?

DNS resolves the name, a TCP connection and TLS handshake are made (or reused), the HTTP request is sent through load balancers to a server, which responds; the browser parses and renders, fetching more resources.

Basic What does Cache-Control: max-age=3600 mean?

The response is fresh for an hour and may be reused without contacting the server.

Basic What is a CDN?

A geographically distributed network of caching proxies that serves content from locations near users, reducing latency and origin load.

Medium How do ETag and 304 work?

The server sends an ETag identifying the version; later the client asks conditionally with If-None-Match, and an unchanged resource returns 304 with no body.

Medium What is the best caching strategy for static assets?

Content-hashed file names with long immutable max-age, so changes get new URLs and never need purging, while HTML stays short-lived.

Medium Why can caching authenticated pages in a shared cache be dangerous?

One user may receive another's personalised response. Use private or no-store and correct Vary headers.

Medium HTTP/1.1 versus HTTP/2 versus HTTP/3?

1.1 handles one request per connection at a time; 2 multiplexes streams over one TCP connection with header compression; 3 runs over QUIC (UDP) removing TCP head-of-line blocking and speeding up handshakes.

Medium Polling, long polling, SSE and WebSocket: when each?

Polling for simple low-frequency needs, long polling as a fallback, SSE for one-way server push, WebSocket for bidirectional low-latency messaging.

Medium How do you scale WebSocket servers?

Keep a registry of which gateway holds each user, route messages via pub/sub, load-balance at L4 or WebSocket-aware L7, drain connections on deploy and use jittered reconnects.

Advanced What does TLS give you and what is forward secrecy?

Encryption, integrity and server authentication. Forward secrecy uses ephemeral key exchange so recorded traffic stays secret even if long-term keys leak later.

Advanced What is an origin shield and request coalescing?

A mid-tier cache that collapses many edge misses into one origin request, and coalescing sends a single origin fetch when many users ask for the same uncached object.

Advanced How do you improve Largest Contentful Paint?

Serve HTML fast from the edge, optimise and preload the hero image, inline critical CSS, defer blocking scripts and reduce server response time.

Advanced How can DNS be used for traffic management, and what are its limits?

Geo or latency routing, weighted records and health-checked failover, limited by TTL caching and resolvers that ignore low TTLs, so failover is slow compared with anycast or load balancers.

Advanced Why do HTTP caches need Vary?

Vary lists request headers that change the response (such as Accept-Encoding or language) so the cache key includes them and serves the right variant.

SRE and operations (14)

Basic SLI, SLO and SLA?

SLI is the measured indicator, SLO the internal target for it, SLA the contractual promise with penalties.

Basic What is an error budget?

The allowed unreliability, 1 minus the SLO. If budget remains you can ship risky changes; when exhausted you prioritise reliability work.

Medium How many minutes of downtime does 99.9% allow per 30 days?

About 43 minutes (0.1% of 43,200 minutes).

Medium What is burn rate and how do you alert on it?

Error rate divided by the allowed error rate. Page when a high burn rate is seen over both a long and a short window; ticket for slow burns.

Medium Why not alert on CPU usage?

It is a cause, not a user-visible symptom; alert on symptoms such as errors and latency, and keep cause metrics for diagnosis.

Medium Why keep utilisation around 60 to 70%?

Queueing delay rises sharply near saturation and you need headroom for spikes and failures.

Medium State Little's Law with an example.

Average items in system = arrival rate x average time in system; 2,000 requests/s at 50 ms means about 100 in flight.

Medium What roles exist during an incident?

Incident commander, operations lead, communications lead and scribe; the commander coordinates rather than debugging.

Medium What belongs in a blameless postmortem?

Impact, timeline, contributing factors, what went well and badly, and owned, dated action items, without blaming individuals.

Advanced What is coordinated omission in load testing?

A closed-loop client that waits for slow responses sends fewer requests during stalls, hiding the latency real arriving users would see. Use open-loop arrival schedules.

Advanced How do you test resilience?

Load, stress, soak and spike tests, failure injection and chaos experiments with a limited blast radius, game days, and canary or shadow traffic for releases.

Advanced RED versus USE methods?

RED for services: Rate, Errors, Duration. USE for resources: Utilisation, Saturation, Errors.

Advanced What is toil and how do you handle it?

Manual, repetitive, automatable work that scales with the service. Cap it, automate it and use runbooks and self-healing.

Advanced How do you plan capacity for a launch?

Forecast demand, load test per-unit capacity, add N+1 or N+2 headroom for failures and deployments, check dependency quotas and plan autoscaling lead time and cost.

Case studies 7 (14)

Basic How do you compute trending topics over the last hour?

Count per minute buckets, maintain the sliding window by adding the new bucket and removing the oldest, keep top-K per partition and merge, and score growth relative to a baseline.

Medium Why use a Count-Min Sketch for trending?

It tracks approximate frequencies of billions of distinct items in fixed memory with overestimate-only error, paired with a heap for the top items.

Medium Why is greedy nearest-courier matching suboptimal?

It can use up the only good courier for another order; batched assignment minimises total cost over the group.

Medium What services make up a food-delivery platform?

Location, order, dispatch, ETA, tracking, payments and notifications, with an event stream connecting them.

Medium How do you prevent two simultaneous auction bids both winning?

Serialise bids per auction through a single ordered writer or atomic conditional update that checks the current highest bid and increment.

Medium What is anti-sniping?

Extending the end time when a bid arrives near the end, so others can respond.

Medium How does email delivery handle failures?

Store-and-forward queues with retries and backoff on temporary (4xx) errors for days and bounces on permanent (5xx) errors.

Medium What are SPF, DKIM and DMARC?

SPF lists servers allowed to send for a domain, DKIM signs messages with the domain's key, and DMARC sets policy and reporting based on both.

Advanced How does Slack-style chat avoid per-member fan-out?

Gateways subscribe to channels for their connected users, so one publish reaches each gateway with an interested user; offline users read from the log and unread counts are last-read pointers.

Advanced Why store the last read message id instead of read flags?

One small pointer per user per channel replaces millions of per-message rows, and unread is latest id minus pointer.

Advanced How does presence scale for large channels?

Heartbeats with TTL in a fast store, broadcasting changes only to interested peers in batches, and not showing presence for huge channels.

Advanced How would you design the ETA service?

Prep time plus courier-to-restaurant plus restaurant-to-customer travel from a road graph with live traffic, corrected by a model trained on historical delivery outcomes.

Advanced How do you handle a courier who stops sending location?

Mark the courier stale after missed pings, alert the customer if needed, and re-dispatch the order.

Advanced How would you detect bidding fraud?

Pattern analysis of bidder-seller relationships, new account behaviour, bid timing and retractions, plus payment verification for high-value items.

Client and mobile design (14)

Basic What does offline-first mean?

The UI reads from a local database and writes locally first, and a sync engine reconciles with the server in the background.

Basic Why queue actions with client-generated ids?

So retries after timeouts are idempotent and duplicates are ignored by the server.

Medium How do you sync after being offline for days?

Use a cursor or sequence token to fetch changes in pages, apply tombstones for deletes, and fall back to a full resync if the token is too old.

Medium Why are tombstones needed?

Without them an offline device cannot learn about a deletion and may push the old item back.

Medium How do you resolve conflicting offline edits?

Choose by data meaning: last write wins for trivial fields, field-level three-way merge, operation-based merge for counters and lists, CRDTs for collaboration, or ask the user.

Medium Why can't you rely on push notifications for correctness?

Delivery is best effort and can be delayed, dropped or throttled; the app must be able to fetch the data itself.

Medium How would you upload a large video from a phone?

Resumable chunked upload directly to object storage with a pre-signed URL, verify checksums, then process asynchronously.

Medium What is adaptive bitrate streaming?

Video is transcoded into several renditions and cut into short segments; the player picks a rendition each segment based on measured throughput.

Advanced How do you design the API to support old app versions?

Version the API, add only optional fields, ignore unknown fields, keep behaviour for minimum supported versions, use feature flags and force upgrades only as a last resort.

Advanced How do you reduce battery drain from networking?

Batch requests, avoid polling, use push and OS-scheduled background work, compress, and respect low-power and data-saver modes.

Advanced How do you handle token storage and refresh on mobile?

Store tokens in the keychain or keystore, use short-lived access tokens with rotating refresh tokens, and handle revocation gracefully.

Advanced How do you prevent out-of-memory crashes from images?

Request right-sized images from a resizing CDN, downsample bitmaps to view size, use bounded memory and disk caches, and avoid holding full-size decoded images.

Advanced What metrics matter for mobile client observability?

Startup time, frame drops, crash-free sessions, network failures by connection type, API latency from the client and battery impact.

Advanced How do silent pushes and background sync interact?

A data push can wake the app to sync, but the OS throttles frequency and execution time, so the app must tolerate missed or delayed wake-ups.

Search and data modelling (14)

Basic What is an inverted index?

A mapping from each term to the documents (and positions) that contain it, so queries intersect postings lists instead of scanning text.

Basic What is text analysis in search?

Tokenising and normalising text (lowercase, stop words, stemming, synonyms) the same way at index and query time.

Medium Explain TF-IDF and BM25.

TF rewards terms frequent in a document, IDF rewards terms rare across the corpus. BM25 adds saturating term frequency and document length normalisation with parameters k1 and b.

Medium How does a distributed search query execute?

Scatter to all shards, each returns local top-k with scores, and the coordinator merges them; replicas add availability and throughput.

Medium Why is search eventually consistent with the primary database?

Updates flow asynchronously through CDC or a queue and become visible after the next refresh; the database remains the source of truth.

Medium How would you implement autocomplete?

A trie or edge n-gram index keyed by prefix, with candidates ranked by popularity and recency, cached and updated from query logs.

Medium How do you add typo tolerance?

Fuzzy matching with edit distance via automata or n-gram candidates, phonetic algorithms for names, and query suggestions from term statistics.

Medium Compare multi-tenancy models.

Shared tables with tenant_id are cheapest but need strict filtering; schema per tenant and database per tenant increase isolation and cost; cells or hybrids group tenants by size.

Medium Hierarchy storage patterns?

Adjacency list (parent_id), materialised path, nested sets and closure table, trading read simplicity, subtree queries and move cost.

Advanced How do you reindex without downtime?

Build a new index with the new analyser in parallel, replay or backfill data, keep it updated, then switch an alias atomically.

Advanced Why is deep pagination expensive in search?

Each shard must compute and return all results up to the offset; use search_after cursors or cap the depth.

Advanced How do you model time-series data?

Partition by time, key by series and time, downsample with rollups and retention tiers, and control label cardinality.

Advanced How do you make schema changes with no downtime?

Expand and contract: add compatible structure, dual write, backfill in batches, switch reads, then remove the old structure.

Advanced Soft delete trade-offs?

Recoverability and auditing versus polluted queries, broken unique constraints (use partial indexes) and legal deletion duties; purge on a schedule or use crypto-shredding.

Explained simply (14)

Basic Explain sharding to a non-engineer.

Like splitting a library's books across rooms by author name: each room is smaller and faster to search, but finding things across rooms is harder and some rooms get busier.

Basic Explain caching in one analogy.

Keeping the books you use most on your desk instead of walking to the library; fast, small and sometimes out of date.

Basic What is the difference between replication and sharding?

Replication copies the same data to several nodes for safety and read capacity; sharding splits different data across nodes for capacity.

Basic Explain a load balancer.

A host who seats each party at a table with space and stops seating at tables without a waiter.

Medium Explain CAP with an analogy.

Two bank branches with a broken phone line must either refuse withdrawals until they can talk (consistent) or allow them and reconcile later (available).

Medium Explain consistent hashing in plain words.

Servers and keys are placed on a circle; each key belongs to the next server clockwise, so adding a server moves only the keys between it and its neighbour.

Medium Why do we need idempotency?

Retries after timeouts are unavoidable, and repeating a non-idempotent action such as payment would duplicate its effect.

Medium Explain a queue versus pub/sub versus a log.

A queue gives each message to one worker, pub/sub gives it to every subscriber, a log keeps an ordered history many readers can replay.

Medium Why are backups needed in addition to replication?

Replication faithfully copies mistakes such as deletes and corruption; backups give a point-in-time state to restore.

Medium Why is p99 more useful than the average?

Users and fan-out requests experience the slow tail; averages hide it.

Advanced How do you explain a trade-off to an interviewer?

Name the pain, the tool that addresses it, what it costs, and the condition under which you would choose differently.

Advanced When is a monolith the better choice?

For small teams and early products where a modular monolith is simpler to build, test, deploy and debug; split into services when scale, team or release independence demand it.

Advanced Explain eventual consistency and where it is not acceptable.

Replicas converge if updates stop, fine for likes and feeds; unacceptable for money, inventory counts and uniqueness constraints without extra coordination.

Advanced Give the back-of-the-envelope recipe.

Convert users into per-second rates, rates into servers, rates times size into storage and bandwidth, and state assumptions and peak factors explicitly.
